can it be done?

The Mandelbrot set lives in the complex numbers, and the complex numbers are special: a plane you can add, multiply and divide in. The Mandelbulb was the search for a 3D version, and it had to cheat, because no such number system exists in three dimensions. In four there are the quaternions, the only other one (Frobenius, 1878), but they are the wrong kind: their Mandelbrot set is just the flat one spun round.

So "the Mandelbrot set in 4D" is four different things, each giving something up. Switch between them above. The first is the honest one and, in a way, the oldest: the Mandelbrot set was always a slice of a four-dimensional object. Its other slices are the Julia sets.

Solid shows the 3D slice your frame cuts. Plane shows the 2D slice through the middle of your view, the familiar picture. Cut open takes away the half of the solid toward you, so the cut face is that same plane. The pad twists your slice into w and the lever moves it ana and kata, as in HILBERT⁴. Double-tap to fly to a point. Keys: J L I K twist, R F move, V swaps the view, C cuts it open, Z squares up.

how it's drawn

Each set has a distance estimate: from the orbit's escape speed and its derivative, a lower bound, near enough, on how far a point is from the set. In four dimensions it bounds the distance within your slice too, so the shader sphere-traces your slice by it: step a fraction of the estimate, again, until it's under a pixel. For (c, z₀) the derivative is both ∂z/∂c and ∂z/∂z₀, since z depends on both holomorphically. The bicomplex estimate is the two Mandelbrot sets' estimates combined as a product. The surface's colour is how close the orbit came to 0, or (by w) whether the set carries on ana or kata of your slice there.

Single precision: zoom past about 10⁴× and the picture breaks up into blocks.

Checked: test/mandelbract.selftest.mjs holds the (c, z₀) set to the Mandelbrot set on z₀ = 0 and to known Julia sets on c = 0 and c = −2; the bicomplex orbit to the two complex orbits, exactly; the quaternion set to turning symmetry; the hyperbulb to its invariant w = 0 subspace and mirror symmetry; every estimate to being safe to march by (measured); and the march to stopping on the edge.
