particles that make polytopes

From Jeffrey Ventrella's Particle-hedra: Generating Polyhedra with Inter-Particle Forces (Bridges 2024; his app). Every particle is pulled toward the centre (force −0.2·p) and pushed away from every other within a cut-off distance m, by (1 − d/m)·r. Velocities are damped by 0.97 each step. Drop particles in at random and they settle into an even spread: 4 into a tetrahedron, 6 an octahedron, 12 an icosahedron. But 8 don't make a cube: a square antiprism, a Thomson-problem answer.

The trick for the rest is tiers: several kinds of particle, with pushes that needn't be mutual. Six particles that ignore everyone else make an octahedron; eight more, pushed by the six and by each other, settle in the gaps: the cube. Stack enough tiers and you get Archimedean solids.

One dimension up, the same rules with w let free: 5 particles make the 5-cell, 8 the 16-cell, 120 the 600-cell. 16 do not make a tesseract, just as 8 don't make a cube; but on a 16-cell seed, 16 settle into one. And the 24-cell's vertices are exactly a 16-cell's and a tesseract's, put on one sphere. Left to themselves, 24 particles usually stop just short of the 24-cell; shake them a few times and they find it (it has the lower energy).

how it's identified, and checked

Edges as in the paper: the closest pair sets the edge length, and every pair within 3% of it is an edge. A tier is exactly a known shape when all its distances, sorted and scaled to unit radius, match that shape's to 0.02; it has a shape's structure when its edges make the same graph counts (edges, degrees, triangles, squares) but the distances differ. test/particles.selftest.mjs holds every claim on this page from fixed random seeds, including Ventrella's Figure 8 run with his own numbers: the inner three tiers exact, the outer 24 with the rhombicuboctahedron's structure but rhombic squares. The 120-cell (600 particles on a 600-cell seed) did not settle into shape in our tries; like the paper's empty spheres, it waits for a better balance of forces.
