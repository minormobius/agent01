flying in four dimensions

In four dimensions the ground is three-dimensional. Here it is the surface of a 4D ball, a 3-sphere, 8.2 km round: fly straight and you are home in 8 km, whichever way you set off. Up is straight out from the centre. You can turn in three ways on that floor: left and right, as here, and also ana and kata, toward the floor's third direction. Your eyes see the slice of the world through you that holds forward, right and up; ana is the one direction you can't see.

On a planet 1.3 km in radius the horizon at a hundred metres is already well below level, so the camera looks down by most of that dip; the sun lights one side, and the other is night. Two cities stand on it, from the studio's city series (packages/morph: streets, plots, and buildings rebuilt through seven centuries). A city plan is flat and this ground is 3D, so each is the plan laid in seven layers across the ground's third direction, 64 m apart: fly along a layer and you are in the studio's city; turn ana and you cut across the layers, with avenues between.

FLY gives you three cues to ana. Ghosts: the skyline of the land 150 m ana of you is drawn in teal, 150 m kata in rose: a hill you're about to turn into, before you can see it. Light: the sun shines partly from ana, so slopes that face ana are brighter (and ana tint colours the land by which way it climbs along your ana). And the map: the ground round you laid flat from where you are (forward up the map, your right and ana across it), 3 km across, with the peaks, the cities, your track, the beacons, and the square of the plane you're looking along.

The beacons hang in 4D. One shows in your view only as the slice of it you're in: a full disc when you're level with it in ana, shrinking as you're off it, and a dashed ring (teal: it's ana of you; rose: kata) when you've missed its slice entirely. The bar on the right says how far ana or kata the next one is. Turn ana or kata to bring it into your slice, then fly through it.

controls, and how it's drawn

Keys: arrows pitch and turn; A/D turn kata and ana; Q/E swing your wings (turn right toward ana, so the slice you see swings round your nose); W/S throttle. Touch: the stick bottom left, the lever bottom right turns ana and kata, the swing buttons beside it. Drag the map to turn it.

The view is Voxel Space, as in Comanche (1992) and the studio's colour cycle: each of 480 columns walks a ray out over the land in your slice, and every step that rises above what's drawn fills the column to it, from a 256-colour palette (8 materials × 32 shades), lit by the 4D normal, hazed with distance. Each column's ray follows the great circle it runs along, so the curve of the planet is exact. The land is 4D value noise sampled on the sphere (no seams), kept on eight 56³ grids, one per cell of a tesseract (the 4D cube map). test/fly.selftest.mjs checks the grids against the noise, the curved renderer's skyline against a finely marched ray, that flying straight comes home after 8.2 km, that the frame stays square and you never fly under the ground, the beacons, and the cities.
