flying in four dimensions

In four dimensions the ground is three-dimensional: a height over a 3D floor, with the sky above it in the fourth direction, up. You can turn in three ways on that floor: left and right, as here, and also ana and kata, toward the floor's third direction. Your eyes see the slice of the world through you that holds forward, right and up; ana is the one direction you can't see.

So FLY gives you three cues to it. Ghosts: the skyline of the land 150 m ana of you is drawn in teal, 150 m kata in rose: a hill you're about to turn into, before you can see it. Light: the sun shines partly from ana, so slopes that face ana are brighter (and ana tint colours the land by which way it climbs along your ana). And the map: the 3D floor round you, 4 km across, with the peaks, your track, the beacons, and the square of the plane you're looking along.

The beacons hang in 4D. One shows in your view only as the slice of it you're in: a full disc when you're level with it in ana, shrinking as you're off it, and a dashed ring (teal: it's ana of you; rose: kata) when you've missed its slice entirely. The bar on the right says how far ana or kata the next one is. Turn ana or kata to bring it into your slice, then fly through it.

controls, and how it's drawn

Keys: arrows pitch and turn; A/D turn kata and ana; Q/E swing your wings (turn right toward ana, so the slice you see swings round your nose); W/S throttle. Touch: the stick bottom left, the lever bottom right turns ana and kata, the swing buttons beside it. Drag the map to turn it.

The view is Voxel Space, as in Comanche (1992) and the studio's colour cycle: each of 480 columns walks a ray out over the land in your slice, and every step that rises above what's drawn fills the column to it, from a 256-colour palette (8 materials × 32 shades), lit by the 4D normal, hazed with distance. The land is 3D value noise on a 128³ grid, 8 km a side, wrapping, so it never ends; test/fly.selftest.mjs checks it tiles, that the fast grid equals the noise, that the renderer's skyline matches a ray traced finely, that the flight frame stays square and you never fly under the ground, and the beacon geometry.
