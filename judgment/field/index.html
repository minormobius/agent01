The field: who else is working on this

Compiled 28 November 2026. Every link was fetched, or its DOI confirmed through Crossref, except one, which is marked.

The sources are mostly the deep background. This page is the live conversation: people who, between 2023 and 2026, have studied what happens to human judgment when the work is delegated to AI.

Nobody has framed it quite the way the thesis does, as a single loop in which delegation removes the feedback that calibration needs. But the pieces are being measured from six directions, and most of what has been found fits. Each entry says whether it agrees, complicates or cuts against the thesis.

The three closest

Grunde-McLaughlin, Mozannar, Amershi et al., Overseeing Agents Without Constant Oversight (Microsoft Research, 2026)

This is the thesis’s exact situation: a person checking an agent’s work afterwards, from a summarised trace. A better trace interface made supervisors faster and more confident but not more accurate, and their idea of “correct” shifted as they went. That is P1 measured directly. Feedback produced by the supervised system did not train the supervisor. arXiv 2602.16844

Shankar et al., Who Validates the Validators? (UIST 2024)

This paper names the mechanism: criteria drift. You need criteria to grade outputs, but you only form criteria by grading outputs. An evaluator who stops seeing outputs stops forming criteria. That is P2 and P4 in one finding, and their tool, EvalGen, is a working design that turns grading into criterion formation. arXiv 2404.12272

Fernandes et al. (2026), read together with Mozannar et al. (2022–23)

Fernandes: AI help raised people’s test scores, but they overestimated how well they had done, and neither AI literacy nor money fixed it. Mozannar: calibrated reliance can be taught, by onboarding people with examples of where the AI fails. Together they show the gap exists and can be closed by design. This is the developmental half of the programme, already prototyped. Fernandes · Mozannar

What counts against the thesis

  • The protocol may matter more than the person. In AI safety, weak-to-strong generalization (Burns et al., 2023) and debate (Michael et al., 2023; Khan et al., 2024) show oversight can exceed the overseer’s own competence when the process makes the workers produce adversarial evidence. If so, the limit on delegation is the protocol, not the supervisor’s calibration.
  • Miscalibration runs both ways. Radiologists given good AI underweighted it and did no better (Agarwal et al., 2023). The fix is calibration, not more scepticism.
  • Sometimes deferring wins. Recruiters given a better AI did worse by one reading, because their interventions pulled accuracy down (Dell’Acqua, 2022). When the AI is good enough, calibration may only matter for the tail of cases it gets wrong.
  • Maybe ordinary use is enough. Experienced Claude Code users both delegate and interrupt more (Anthropic, 2026). Oversight skill may build without designed feedback, though the data measure trust, not correctness.
  • Some deskilling evidence is fragile. The colonoscopy study is observational and contested. The Anthropic skill-formation RCT has about four people per interaction pattern.

What this changes in the thesis

  1. Widen the claim to include protocols. The strongest counter-evidence doesn’t refute the thesis; it relocates part of it. Debate is a system that produces independent evidence for its supervisor, which is exactly what the thesis asks for. Revised: leverage scales when the work produces feedback that is independent of it, whether that feedback trains the supervisor (developmental) or stands in for the supervisor’s calibration (protocol). Which of the two dominates is an empirical question, and E2 on the testbed can start to answer it.
  2. Calibration means both directions. The measures already count over- and under-supervision. The radiology and recruiter studies say both are common, so neither can be the default fix.
  3. The apprenticeship gap has an economics. Garicano and Rayo model when apprenticeships stay viable once AI does the junior work. Brynjolfsson et al. see the bottom rung thinning in payroll data. The “ascend faster than apprenticeship” claim in the abstract now has a formal model and early evidence behind it.
  4. Design decides. Bastani et al. (guardrailed tutor) and Shen & Tamkin (engaged interaction patterns) both find that the same tool deskills or doesn’t depending on how it is used. That supports treating cultivation as a design problem, not a matter of character.

Supervising agents in practice

The newest empirical work: how people actually oversee agents that work out of sight, and whether better traces make that oversight any better.

  • Madeleine Grunde-McLaughlin, Hussein Mozannar, Saleema Amershi et al.

    Microsoft Research AI Frontiers / University of Washington

    Agrees Across three user studies on a computer-use agent, a better trace interface cut the time spent finding errors and raised confidence, but final accuracy did not improve, and correctness criteria were subjective and shifted. Supervisors grow more confident without growing more correct.

    • Overseeing Agents Without Constant Oversight: Challenges and Opportunities arXiv 2602.16844, 2026

  • Shipi Dhanorkar, Samir Passi, Mihaela Vorvoreanu

    Microsoft (Aether / Responsible AI)

    Agrees Interviews with 17 experienced developers found four forms of oversight work (a priori control, co-planning, real-time monitoring, post hoc review) and home-made heuristics for them, which treats oversight as a practice people learn but no one trains.

    • Human oversight of agentic systems in practice: Examining the oversight work, challenges, and heuristics of developers using software agents arXiv 2606.05391, 2026

  • Anthropic Societal Impacts

    Anthropic

    Complicates Claude Code users move from about 20% to over 40% full auto-approve as they gain experience, yet they also interrupt more. Supervision does change with exposure, but the data show trust and interruption rates, not whether the supervisors were right.

    • Measuring AI agent autonomy in practice Anthropic research report, 2026

  • Andrej Karpathy

    independent (Eureka Labs); formerly OpenAI, Tesla

    Agrees The autonomy slider and the instruction to keep the generation-verification loop fast and the AI on a tight leash are a practitioner's version of the thesis: you should delegate only as far as you can verify quickly.

    • Software Is Changing (Again) Y Combinator AI Startup School talk, 2025

  • Simon Willison

    independent practitioner (Datasette; Django co-creator)

    Agrees Argues that productive agent use is senior-engineer work (tests, review, specs, judging output), so the leverage belongs to people whose judgment was already trained without agents. That leaves open how the next cohort gets that judgment.

    • Vibe engineering simonwillison.net, 2025
    • Vibe coding and agentic engineering simonwillison.net, 2026

Metacognition and calibration with generative AI

Users of generative AI misjudge both the output and their own performance; the work sits between HCI and cognitive psychology.

  • Lev Tankelevitch, Viktor Kewenig, Auste Simkute, Ava Elizabeth Scott, Advait Sarkar, Abigail Sellen, Sean Rintel

    Microsoft Research Cambridge

    Agrees Recasts generative-AI use as a metacognitive task: monitoring, evaluating and calibrating your confidence in output. It updates Bainbridge's ironies of automation, where users shift from producing work to evaluating it and lose the practice that made them good evaluators.

    • The Metacognitive Demands and Opportunities of Generative AI CHI 2024, 2024
    • Ironies of Generative AI: Understanding and Mitigating Productivity Loss in Human-AI Interaction (Simkute et al.) International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction, 2024

  • Hao-Ping (Hank) Lee, Advait Sarkar, Lev Tankelevitch, Ian Drosos, Sean Rintel, Richard Banks, Nicholas Wilson

    Microsoft Research / Carnegie Mellon

    Agrees In a survey of 319 knowledge workers, higher confidence in the AI went with less critical thinking and higher self-confidence went with more. Critical effort shifts toward verification and stewardship, which is the supervisor role the thesis describes.

    • The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers CHI 2025, 2025

  • Daniela Fernandes, Steeven Villa, Salla Nicholls, Otso Haavisto, Daniel Buschek, Albrecht Schmidt, Thomas Kosch, Chenxinran Shen, Robin Welsch

    Aalto University / LMU Munich / HU Berlin

    Agrees AI help raised LSAT scores but participants overestimated their own performance, and neither higher AI literacy nor a monetary incentive fixed the overestimation. This is direct evidence that working through AI degrades calibration even while it improves output.

    • AI makes you smarter but none the wiser: The disconnect between performance and metacognition Computers in Human Behavior (arXiv 2409.16708), 2026

  • Shreya Shankar, J.D. Zamfirescu-Pereira, Björn Hartmann, Aditya Parameswaran, Ian Arawjo

    UC Berkeley / Université de Montréal

    Agrees Documents criteria drift: evaluators need criteria to grade outputs, but they form those criteria by grading outputs. The paper makes the feedback-forms-judgment mechanism concrete and argues it should be designed for.

    • Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences UIST 2024, 2024

  • Hussein Mozannar, David Sontag et al.

    MIT (now Microsoft Research)

    Agrees Shows that calibrated reliance can be taught: onboarding with exemplars and learned rules about where the AI fails improves when people defer and when they override. This is the closest existing prototype of a system that produces training for its supervisor.

    • Effective Human-AI Teams via Learned Natural Language Rules and Onboarding NeurIPS 2023, 2023
    • Teaching Humans When To Defer to a Classifier via Exemplars AAAI 2022, 2022

Deskilling and the broken apprenticeship

Evidence that AI assistance erodes the skill that supervision depends on, and theory of why the training ladder may collapse.

  • Matt Beane

    UC Santa Barbara (Technology Management); MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy

    Agrees Robotic surgery removed novices from the work, and they learned only through unsanctioned shadow learning. The finding is that skill needs challenge, complexity and connection, the same feedback that delegation strips out.

    • The Skill Code: How to Save Human Ability in an Age of Intelligent Machines HarperCollins, 2024
    • Shadow Learning: Building Robotic Surgical Skill When Approved Means Fail Administrative Science Quarterly, 2019

  • Judy Hanwen Shen, Alex Tamkin

    Anthropic (Anthropic Fellows Program)

    Agrees In an RCT (n≈51) on an unfamiliar Python library, AI users scored about 50% on a later quiz against about 65% without AI, with no significant time saving. Three of the six interaction patterns that kept users cognitively engaged preserved learning, so how a tool is used determines whether it deskills.

    • How AI Impacts Skill Formation arXiv 2601.20245, 2026
    • AI assistance and coding skills (companion post) Anthropic research, 2026

  • Krzysztof Budzyń, Marcin Romańczyk, Yuichi Mori et al.

    multicentre (Poland, Norway, Japan, UK)

    Agrees After routine use of AI polyp detection, experienced endoscopists' adenoma detection without AI fell from 28.4% to 22.4%, an early field sign of skill decay among experts. The study is observational and the finding is contested.

    • Endoscopist deskilling risk after exposure to artificial intelligence in colonoscopy: a multicentre, observational study Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology, 2025

  • Hamsa Bastani, Osbert Bastani, Alp Sungu et al.

    Wharton / Penn

    Agrees In a field experiment with about 1,000 students, GPT-4 access raised practice scores but lowered exam scores once access was removed, and a tutor version with guardrails mostly removed the harm. The tool's design decides whether practice builds skill.

    • Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics PNAS, 2025

  • Luis Garicano, Luis Rayo; Enrique Ide

    LSE / Kellogg Northwestern; IESE

    Agrees Juniors pay for training with menial work; once AI does that work, apprenticeships stay viable only if the expertise leverage ratio exceeds a threshold (e). The model explains why delegation can destroy the pipeline that produces future supervisors.

    • Training in the Age of AI: A Theory of Apprenticeship Viability CEPR Discussion Paper 20634, 2025
    • Automation, AI, and the Intergenerational Transmission of Knowledge (Ide) arXiv 2507.16078, 2025

  • Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, Ruyu Chen

    Stanford Digital Economy Lab

    Agrees Early-career workers in AI-exposed occupations are 16–19% below trend, mostly through reduced hiring, while experienced workers are not, which is the bottom rung of the training ladder disappearing in payroll data. The evidence is descriptive, not causal.

    • Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence Stanford Digital Economy Lab working paper, 2025 (rev. 2026)

Productivity, overreliance and self-misjudgment in field and lab experiments

Randomised evidence that the people being helped cannot tell where the AI's competence ends, and that a better AI can make the human worse.

  • METR (Joel Becker, Nate Rush, Beth Barnes, David Rein)

    METR

    Agrees Sixteen experienced developers on their own repositories were 19% slower with AI while believing beforehand they would be 24% faster and afterwards that they had been 20% faster. This is a clean measurement of a miscalibrated supervisor who receives no corrective feedback.

    • Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity arXiv 2507.09089, 2025

  • Fabrizio Dell'Acqua, Edward McFowland III, Ethan Mollick, Hila Lifshitz-Assaf, Karim Lakhani et al.

    Harvard Business School / Wharton / BCG

    Agrees BCG consultants given GPT-4 got much better on tasks inside the frontier and 19 points worse on a task outside it. Separately, recruiters given a better AI deliberated less and did worse than those given a weaker one. Supervisors cannot see where the frontier is, and good AI stops them looking.

    • Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality Organization Science (SSRN 4573321), 2023 (pub. 2026)
    • Falling Asleep at the Wheel: Human/AI Collaboration in a Field Experiment on HR Recruiters (Dell'Acqua) working paper, 2022

  • Nicholas Otis, Rowan Clarke, Solène Delecourt, David Holtz, Rembrand Koning

    UC Berkeley / Harvard Business School

    Agrees With the same GPT-4 mentor, high-performing entrepreneurs gained about 15% and low performers lost about 10%, and the gap came from which advice each group chose to act on. Returns to AI were bounded by the judgment of whoever filtered it.

    • The Uneven Impact of Generative Artificial Intelligence on Entrepreneurial Performance: Evidence from a Field Experiment in Kenya Management Science, 2026

  • Nikhil Agarwal, Alex Moehring, Pranav Rajpurkar, Tobias Salz

    MIT / Harvard

    Complicates Radiologists did not over-rely on AI; they underweighted it relative to their own signal (automation neglect) and on average did not improve. Miscalibration can run in the conservative direction too, so the fix is calibration, not simply more scepticism.

    • Combining Human Expertise with Artificial Intelligence: Experimental Evidence from Radiology NBER Working Paper 31422, 2023

Scalable oversight in AI safety

The same problem framed from the model side: how a weaker supervisor gets reliable signal about a stronger system's work.

  • Samuel Bowman et al.; Julian Michael et al.

    Anthropic / NYU

    Agrees The sandwiching paradigm measures whether non-experts assisted by a model can supervise beyond their own competence, and human debate studies find they can when the protocol forces adversarial evidence, which is structured feedback that the supervised system is made to produce.

    • Measuring Progress on Scalable Oversight for Large Language Models arXiv 2211.03540, 2022
    • Debate Helps Supervise Unreliable Experts arXiv 2311.08702, 2023

  • Akbir Khan, John Hughes, Ethan Perez et al.

    UCL / Anthropic / FAR

    Agrees Non-expert human judges reached 88% accuracy with debate (model judges 76%), against much lower baselines. A process in which the workers argue against each other can produce the feedback a weak supervisor needs.

    • Debating with More Persuasive LLMs Leads to More Truthful Answers ICML 2024, 2024

  • Collin Burns, Jan Leike, Ilya Sutskever et al.

    OpenAI Superalignment

    Complicates Strong models trained on a weak supervisor's labels can outperform the supervisor, so in the model case oversight does not have to be as capable as the system it oversees. That is the main counterweight to the thesis's claim that delegation is capped by the supervisor's calibration.

    • Weak-to-Strong Generalization: Eliciting Strong Capabilities With Weak Supervision arXiv 2312.09390 (ICML 2024), 2023

Judgment economics and joint cognitive systems

Theory of why judgment becomes the scarce input, and the human-factors tradition that saw the ironies first.

  • Ajay Agrawal, Joshua Gans, Avi Goldfarb

    University of Toronto (Rotman)

    Complicates Judgment is the complement to cheap prediction, and in the pricing paper it is learned from experience, but better prediction moves the returns to different kinds of judgment in opposite directions. Not all judgment gains value, and the models do not ask where calibrated judgment comes from.

    • Exploring the Impact of Artificial Intelligence: Prediction versus Judgment Information Economics and Policy (NBER w24626), 2019
    • Human Judgment and AI Pricing NBER Working Paper 24284 / AEA P&P, 2018

  • David Autor, Neil Thompson

    MIT

    Complicates Whether automation helps or hurts a worker depends on whether it removes the inexpert or the expert tasks of the job. Delegation can raise the value of remaining expertise while removing the tasks that used to build it.

    • Expertise NBER Working Paper 33941, 2025

  • David D. Woods

    Ohio State University (Cognitive Systems Engineering)

    Agrees From the joint-cognitive-systems tradition: as more authority is delegated to more capable agents, they will misbehave in ways that look strong and wrong, so the system must be designed for joint activity and directable observability rather than for autonomy alone.

    • When AI Agents Misbehave and Fail to Coordinate: Architecting for Joint Activity seminar slides, SINTEF MARMAN seminar, 2025 link not directly checked
