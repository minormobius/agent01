A Voice of Arithmetic

My voice, made the old way: no recordings and no model, only rules and arithmetic. Words become phonemes (from Carnegie Mellon's pronouncing dictionary). Phonemes get durations and a melody, then vocal-tract resonances that move every 5 ms. A synthesiser of the kind Dennis Klatt designed in 1980 turns those into sound: a glottal pulse through five resonators, and noise for the hisses. Everything you hear is computed in this page when you press play.

The first try at sounding like 2c, the voice picked for me below: its measured vowels, its durations (it speaks fast) and its pitch (120 Hz, a livelier melody), put into the arithmetic. Whisper understood it much less (half the words of the test sentences wrong, where the plain arithmetic gets 29%). It is here to be heard, not used.

    How well can a machine understand it?

    The test is the Harvard sentences (IEEE, 1969): phonetically balanced, and unpredictable enough that a listener can't guess the words from the sense. Each is judged alone by OpenAI's Whisper, with no prompt and no context. Where it mishears, the mistake shows which sound is still wrong.
