〜 Jurassic

Daohugou, ~165 Ma — a forest you can walk around in, in the dark, by ear ↩ mino.mobi

Nine ensiferans sang in this forest. Eight of them in pure tone around 5 kHz, and one — Sigmaboilus peregrinus — above 20 kHz, which is the oldest ultrasonic communication known from any animal, and a hundred and thirteen million years before the first bat. Press play and walk about. Everything you hear is synthesised from the instrument: a tooth-strike train driving a wing resonator, then thrown across the air by ISO 9613 with the distance, the temperature and the leaves all taken off it.

The gap this closes

Katydid ultrasound has usually been explained as an answer to bats. These fossils are 113 million years too early for that, which is the 2026 paper's point and the reason the who is listening control above matters more than it looks. Switch between an early mammal and an archosaur and watch the ultrasonic circle swell and vanish: on one reading ultrasound bought no privacy at all, on the other it bought a private channel. The fossils do not settle it. The physics only tells you what each answer costs.

What is measured here, and what is not

Two numbers on this page are published results. Archaboilus musicus sang at 6.4 kHz — Gu, Engel & Ren 2012, PNAS 109:3868, from a file of 107 teeth over 9.34 mm. Sigmaboilus peregrinus sang above 20 kHz — Gu et al. 2026, PNAS 123(36):e2615107123, the paper this surface was built for. That paper was paywalled when this was written; its per-species table has not been transcribed here, and the roster below is the described singing ensiferans of the Daohugou beds rather than a copy of its particular nine.

Every other frequency is ours — modelled from how stridulation works and from the shape of the assemblage as described, and labelled modelled wherever it appears. The Jurassic audiograms are worse than that: no ear fossilises, so they are labelled hypothesis and are best read as three positions in an argument you can switch between.

The propagation is not a guess. Spreading, atmospheric absorption and foliage scattering are ISO 9613-1:1993 and ISO 9613-2:1996, implemented in Rust and checked against the standard's own published table at four temperature–humidity conditions across eight octaves. That test runs in CI. If it ever fails, every radius on this page is wrong and the build goes red.

Sound and physics: a zero-dependency Rust kernel compiled to raw wasm32-unknown-unknown, run from an AudioWorklet — no wasm-bindgen, no allocator on the audio thread. The kernel knows nothing about insects; the roster is one editable file. Source: README. Plot ?plot= is a permalink — the same number grows the same forest anywhere.
