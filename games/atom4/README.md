# ATOM⁴

**The official position on the universe we're building**: four dimensions of
space, with the force between charges pinned at 1/r. It's an understanding toy
like Lumen⁴, not a game. 3D is warm and 4D is ice.

`index.html` is the position: every conclusion, one card per chapter, and the
open questions. Then seven chapter pages, each showing its working:

## The premise

Gauss's law in 4D makes the potential of a charge fall as 1/r². In the
radial equation that's the same power as the centrifugal term, so nothing
sets a length. Below a critical coupling there is no bound state, and above
it the electron falls in. There's no atom either way (Ehrenfest, 1917, for
the classical orbits). So the potential is **pinned at 1/r** in four
dimensions. Section 7 of the page asks what that costs.

## The solver

| piece | how |
|---|---|
| one electron | u = r^((d−1)/2) R satisfies the 3D radial equation with l → L = l + (d − 3)/2. On x = ln r with u = r^½ y: y″ = [2r²(V − ε) + (L + ½)²] y, Numerov on 700 points, r from 10⁻⁵ to 150 bohr |
| its energy | bisection on the outward solution's node count (Sturm). The lower bound is the bare nucleus's level, since the cloud only repels. Integration stops where h²f/12 > ½: past that Numerov flips sign every step and fakes nodes. That was the bug that broke 3D s-states at first |
| its wavefunction | outward to the classical turning point, inward from where Numerov is still stable, spliced |
| the cloud's pull | a unit charge spread over a sphere of radius s, felt at r: 1/max(r, s) in 3D (the shell theorem); k(min/max)/max in 4D, k(t) = (2/π)∫₀^π sin²θ dθ/√(1 + t² − 2t cos θ), tabulated |
| many electrons | Hartree: each subshell solved in the nucleus's pull plus the whole cloud minus its own one-electron share; mixed 0.4 per iteration. E = Σ o ε − ½ΣΣ o o′ J + ½ Σ o J_self |
| the ground configuration | fill by the orbital energies of every subshell in the current field until it stops changing, then try moving one or two electrons among the open, top-filled and lowest-empty subshells, keeping the least total energy |
| ionization | ΔSCF: the relaxed ion's energy minus the atom's, taking the electron from whichever of the three least-bound subshells leaves the ion lowest |

## What it finds

- **Hydrogen**: E = −Z²/2(n + ½)², ground 6.05 eV, mean radius 3 bohr. Shells hold 2, 10, 28, 60, 110
  electrons ((l + 1)² orbitals per l). Its fall to n = 2 is red to orange, ending at 570 nm.
- **The 3D check**: 34 of Z = 1–36 match our real configurations. Chromium and copper don't (exchange).
  Helium's energy is −2.86168, Hartree–Fock's exactly. Ionization energies run about a third low.
- **The 4D table**: rows of 2, 10, 10, 28, 28 (Z = 1–78). The noble gases are 2, 12, 22, 50 and 78. Element 7 (s² p³)
  is carbon-like, with five bonds at the corners of a 5-cell (104.48°). The solver puts elements 64–67 in d-rich,
  s-poor configurations, like palladium. Those are the least trustworthy entries.
- **The shell theorem's failure, in the chemistry**: outside a shell k < 1, so inner electrons only partly
  screen the nucleus. 4D noble gases get *harder* to ionize down the column (13.6 → 18.8 eV), where ours get
  easier. With the 3D kernel forced on (`scf(…, { shellTheorem: true })`) the rise is gone. A neutral 4D atom
  pulls on a distant electron with −Σr²/8s³. Ours exerts no pull at all.

## The shapes (chapter 6)

The six regular 4D polytopes, built from coordinates:
- the 5-cell from the simplex;
- the tesseract from (±1)⁴;
- the 16-cell from ±eᵢ;
- the 24-cell from the permutations of (±1, ±1, 0, 0);
- the 600-cell from ±eᵢ, (±½)⁴ and the even permutations of ½(±φ, ±1, ±1/φ, 0);
- the 120-cell as the centres of the 600-cell's 600 tetrahedra.

Everything else is computed:
- edges are the least-distance pairs;
- faces are the planar k-cycles;
- cells are the vertices farthest along each vertex of the dual.

The selftest checks the f-vectors against Schläfli's and V − E + F − C = 0.

A tile draws a scene two ways:
- **shadow**: a perspective camera off along w, coloured kata pink to ana green;
- **slice**: the 3D cross-section at w = h. Each face crossing the hyperplane gives one edge, a ball gives a
  ball of radius √(r² − Δw²), and a bond gives the stretch of it within its radius of the hyperplane.

Drag turns it in 3D or through w. A touch turns it by its sideways drag only, so the page still scrolls.

## Darbon (chapter 7)

Element 7 of the 4D table (s² p³, half a shell) is named darbon.

- **Bonds.** sp⁴ hybrids are a fifth s each (sp³'s are a quarter: a² = 1/(d + 1)) and point to a 5-cell's
  corners, 104.48° apart. Its hydride is DH₅, and chains carry 3n + 2 hydrogens.
- **Handedness.** It's chiral with five different neighbours: the simplex's rotations are exactly its even
  shuffles, checked over all 120. Our own chiral molecules aren't chiral in 4D: half a turn through w is a mirror
  for anything with w = 0, and a tile shows it.
- **Crystals.** Its diamond is two copies of the lattice A₄, one bond apart, by the same construction as
  graphene's honeycomb (A₂) and diamond (A₃, the fcc lattice). It can't come from the hypercube: no tesseract
  corners make a regular 5-cell, checked over all 4368 choices. It has a five-fold rotation, which 4D crystals
  allow and 3D crystals don't.

All of this is hybrid-orbital geometry, not computed bonding.

## Rewriting Maxwell

A static r^−p in D_s dimensions is the transform of 1/(k²)^a with 2a = D_s − p. Maxwell's equations are a = 1: 1/r in 3D, 1/r² in 4D.
Pinning 1/r in 4D needs a = 3/2. The routes and what each one costs:

1. **Local, linear and relativistic**: impossible. □ gives 1/k², □² gives 1/k⁴ (a log in 4D).
2. **□^(3/2), relativistic but nonlocal**: positive probabilities (Källén–Lehmann) need the propagator to be a
   positive sum of 1/(p² + m²), never more singular than 1/p². The field's dimension Δ = 1 is below the
   scalar unitarity bound of 3/2. The result has ghosts, and light becomes a continuum with no photon.
3. **ε(k) = ε₀ℓk, a strange vacuum**: a new length ℓ, and light disperses as ω = c√(k/ℓ) like deep-water
   waves. There's no invariant speed, so relativity is gone.
4. **A nonlinear Gauss law**, ∇·(|E|^(m−2)E) = ρ with m = 5/2: superposition is gone.
5. **A compact fourth dimension** of size ℓ: (π/ℓr) coth(πr/ℓ). That's 1/r² close and 1/r far, but atoms
   bigger than ℓ are 3D atoms.

6. **The lens vacuum, the one this universe takes**: ε(k) = ε₀ℓk *and* μ(k) = μ₀/ℓk. Then E′ = D/ε₀ and
   B′ = μ₀H obey Maxwell's equations in empty 4D space exactly, with the usual sources: that is the light, and
   it's Lumen⁴'s. What pushes charges is E = E′/ℓ|k|, B = B′/ℓ|k|, the light blurred across space by a 1/r³
   kernel at each instant. Statics come out as 1/(ℓk³), the pinned 1/r; waves as ω = ck at every wavelength; the
   energy (E′² + c²B′²)ε₀/2ℓ|k| is never negative; fields add. The cost is relativity for the force: an absolute
   now, a preferred frame.

Every route keeps the shell-theorem problem: ∇²(1/r) = −1/r³ in 4D, flux that grows with radius, and no Faraday
cage (the potential at the centre of a charged shell is 3π/8 of the potential at its wall). The big problem: in
3D, locality, linearity, relativity and unitarity together force 1/r. In 4D they force 1/r², and there are no
atoms. This universe gives up relativity, for electromagnetism only.

## Light (chapter 2)

Under the lens vacuum Lumen⁴'s conclusions hold for the light itself: 1/r³, three polarizations, the wake,
1.43 λ/D, ν⁴ and T⁵, and the rainbow's conics as geometry (its 42° is our water's). Matter feels a
single frequency through the lens as one number, c/ℓω, so every pattern is unchanged; long waves push
harder. A flash, having every k at once, is felt faintly before its front arrives. This is argued, not
yet computed. Small-particle scattering goes as ω^(d+1)(c/ℓω)² = ω³ (ours ω⁴, 4D Maxwell ω⁵); the light
chapter draws the three skies from Lumen⁴'s Planck spectra and colour function, assuming a
frequency-independent polarizability. Lumen⁴'s page carries a note pointing here.

## Motion and spin (chapter 3)

- F = ma in four components. Central-force motion stays in a plane: under 1/r, Kepler's ellipses. The
  Runge–Lenz vector p²x − (x·p)p − x/r is conserved in any dimension. Under Gauss's 1/r³ force a nudged circle
  flies off (Ehrenfest).
- Turns act on six planes. An antisymmetric Ω splits by the Hodge star into self-dual and anti-self-dual halves,
  which commute and each close as so(3).
- **Spin.** In 4 + 1 the Euclidean γ₁…γ₄ give γ₁γ₂γ₃γ₄, which squares to 1 and anticommutes with each, so it
  *is* time's γ₀ (in one of the two irreducible representations). A slow electron (γ₀ = +1) is therefore one
  chirality of Spin(4) = SU(2) × SU(2): two states, turned by the left half of every rotation and blind to the
  right. The selftest builds the γ matrices and checks this. Pairing (two per orbital) assumes the electron is
  the smallest spinor and that spin-statistics holds; the lens vacuum isn't fully relativistic, so both are
  taken as part of the universe's definition. Only the self-dual half of a magnetic field can flip a spin.
- Fine structure: orbitals of angular momentum l are (l/2, l/2), (l + 1)² of them (harmonic polynomials, checked
  against `atom.js`); spin on the left half splits 2(l + 1)² into (l + 1)(l + 2) + l(l + 1).
- A rigid body: M_ab = (j_a + j_b)Ω_ab, dM/dt = [M, Ω] in the body frame, with the energy, tr M² and the
  Pfaffian conserved (checked); integrable (Manakov, 1976). The tile integrates orientation with a
  Cayley step, which stays exactly orthogonal.
- A bond's stabilizer is SO(3): three twist angles. In D₂H₈ each darbon carries four hydrogens.

## What it leaves out

Exchange (so Cr and Cu come out wrong and ionization runs low), non-spherical open shells, relativity
(it matters past Z ≈ 50), spin–orbit in the solver, and correlation. Spin is two states per orbital
(chapter 3 says why). Subshells are limited to l ≤ 3, n ≤ 8, which is enough up to Z = 78. Open questions,
as on the landing: the forces between molecules through the lens (the molecular-dynamics view needs them
first), gravity (it has to be pinned too), exchange, spin-statistics without full relativity, and 4D water.
