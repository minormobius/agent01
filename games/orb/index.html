ORB

Minesweeper with the grid taken away. The world is a sphere of Voronoi cells — fives, sixes, sevens, the odd four — and a number counts the mines in every cell that shares an edge with it. No edges of the world, no corners. Turn it with a finger.

An irregular mesh makes Minesweeper worse at being fair, not better: measured, a random orb needs a coin-flip far more often than a hex grid with the same neighbour count. So nothing here is random in that way. Every orb is played out by an exact solver from your first tap before you see it, and only boards that clear by pure deduction ship.

Which means at every moment, some cell is certain. The game checks each move you make against that and counts the ones that weren't. Zero guesses is the real clear.
