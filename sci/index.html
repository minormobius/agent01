scientific instruments, taken apart

One page per instrument, each built around a solver you can drive rather than an analogy that falls apart when you push on it. If a diagram's only justification is that it is the diagram everyone draws, it does not ship here.

The rule. Every mechanism claim on a page traces to a primary source, and the source is on the page. Every number is computed from constants and closed-form physics in front of you, not quoted — and the solver that computes it is checked against an analytic solution on every build.

Instruments

  • MRI rust → wasm nmr part one
    The most celebrated instrument in applied physics detects its signal with a coil of wire. What that means: Faraday induction in the near field, the principle of reciprocity turning a hard reception problem into a Biot–Savart integral, a coil that goes deaf when you point it down the bore, and why field strength buys signal twice over.
  • k-space rust → wasm fourier part two
    If the sensor has no spatial resolution, where do the pixels come from? The gradients — a steering wheel for frequency space. Run a real acquisition on a phantom whose k-space is evaluated in closed form, delete parts of k-space to see what each was carrying, and watch an EPI image slide off its own outline by exactly the number of pixels the formula predicts.
  • contrast rust → wasm tissue part three
    An MRI does not measure tissue. It measures a tissue's response to a schedule, and somebody picks the schedule. Measured T₁ and T₂ from Stanisz 2005, sequence equations validated against a Bloch simulation rather than a textbook, the Ernst angle, and the curve through TR–TE along which two tissues become invisible to each other.
  • why it screams planned
    Lorentz forces on the gradient windings, and the 110–120 dB that every patient has personally experienced and nobody explains to them.

Why

Most instrument explainers stop at the block diagram. The interesting part is always one level below it — the place where a physical constraint forces the design to be the shape it is. An MRI scanner is enormous and cold and expensive because of a factor of B₀ that belongs to the detector rather than to the physics, which is why ultra-low-field machines that detect differently can be neither. You cannot see that from a block diagram, and you can see it immediately from a graph you are allowed to drag.
