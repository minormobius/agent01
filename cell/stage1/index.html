Stage 1 · stochastic gene expression

One molecule at a time

This is a tiny cell, 400 nm across, with six genes. Inside it, proteins are made and lost through four kinds of event, and each one happens at a random moment. The simulation draws those moments with Gillespie's exact algorithm, one event at a time. That randomness is why real cells of the same type still differ from each other.

How a protein gets made, and lost

1

Transcription

A gene is copied into an mRNA molecule. When that happens is random. On average it's every 50 to 250 seconds, depending on the gene.

2

Translation

Ribosomes read the mRNA and build proteins from it, each at a random moment. An mRNA makes only a handful before it's gone.

3

mRNA decay

mRNA is fragile: half of it is gone within a minute. A short-lived mRNA that makes a few proteins means proteins arrive in bursts.

4

Dilution

Proteins are stable. The cell grows and divides every 105 minutes, which spreads them thinner, so a protein's average stay is about 2.5 hours.

Two extras wire the genes together. Repressor R sits on gene T's DNA and blocks step 1 for T, so more R means less T. Proteins α and β stick together into an αβ complex and sometimes come apart again.

Activity timeline

Each bar is one mRNA molecule, from the moment it was transcribed to the moment it decayed. Each tick on a bar is one protein translated from it. Notice how few proteins each mRNA makes, and how the bars come and go at irregular times: that is where the bursts in the counts come from. Hover a bar for its story.

Three things to try

Watch a burst

Follow Repressor R at 10×. A new R mRNA appears only every few minutes. It makes two or three proteins and vanishes, and R's count jumps by that much at once.

Small numbers are noisy

Compare the sparklines. Elongation factor, at about 950 copies, is nearly flat. Repressor R, at about 95, wobbles by 10% or more. The fewer copies, the bigger the relative noise, and averages hide it.

Cut a wire

Knock out Repressor R at 300×. No new R is made, but existing R only dilutes away, so it takes hours. As R falls, T's gene is freed and T's timeline lane fills with bars.

What is exact

The four events for six genes, the repression of T by R, and α + β binding make 27 reaction channels over 13 species, fired by the Gillespie direct method. Every event is a real random draw from the right distribution, so the bursts and noise come out of the model rather than being drawn in.

What is decorative

The model is well-mixed: it tracks how many of each molecule exist, not where they are. Positions in the picture are a random walk for the eye, and which mRNA a protein came from is assigned at random among the live ones, which is statistically exact. Parameters are illustrative, sized for a JCVI-syn3A-like cell.

What Stage 2 adds

Position. In Stage 2 every molecule is a particle that really diffuses, and where the enzymes sit changes what the cell produces. That's something no well-mixed model like this one can show.
