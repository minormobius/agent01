Minimal Cell Twin

The goal is a living cell you can watch: every protein made, bound and lost as a discrete event, animated as it happens. That is a research problem. The path there is a series of layers, each adding one kind of realism to the one inside it. Every layer stays live, so you can see what each step bought.

1

Stochastic gene expression

Six genes in a 400 nm cell, simulated with Gillespie's exact algorithm. It covers transcription, mRNA decay, translation, dilution, a repressor and a binding pair. Every dot is one protein. Knock out a gene and watch the cell respond. The cell is well-mixed, so position means nothing yet.

2

Where the enzymes sit

Every metabolite is a particle taking Brownian steps, and enzymes capture what drifts close enough. Two cells have the same enzymes and rate constants, and only their arrangement differs. Clustered enzymes keep a leaky intermediate from escaping, which a well-mixed model cannot show.

3

A real organism: JCVI-syn3A

Swap the toy genes for the minimal cell's published genome, copy numbers and kinetics. 3a lays out all 496 genes with their functions and measured protein counts. 3b runs all 455 protein genes as exact events on 187 shared RNA polymerases and 503 ribosomes. 3c puts energy metabolism underneath, so the ribosomes run on glucose and stall when it runs out. 3d makes it grow, copy its chromosome and divide, generation after generation, with cycle times near the lab's 105 minutes.

4

Offline runs, browser playback

A full 105-minute cell cycle at protein resolution is too heavy for a browser in real time. Run it offline instead, in Rust compiled to WebAssembly or an established engine. Then stream the trajectory so you can scrub through a whole life.

5

The twin

A whole-cell model simulates a generic cell. A twin is calibrated to one real culture and keeps updating from measurements such as imaging and omics. That feedback loop between experiment and model is the open research problem.

Research

Why a minimal cell

JCVI-syn3A has about 490 genes, the smallest genome of any free-living cell. It is also the only cell with a published spatially resolved whole-cell model, so the biology doesn't have to be invented.

Why discrete events

Most proteins in a small cell exist in tens to hundreds of copies. At those numbers, noise is a large part of how the cell behaves. Exact stochastic events capture it, and averages hide it.

How it is built

Plain HTML, p5.js for drawing, and simulation engines in Web Workers, with no build step. Each stage is its own page and is never overwritten. A new layer gets a new page.
