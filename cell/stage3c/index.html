Stage 3c · metabolism underneath

Fuel for 503 ribosomes

In 3b, building a protein cost nothing. Here it costs what it costs a real cell: one ATP and two GTP for every amino acid added. The only way syn3A makes that energy is by breaking down glucose. Below, its energy metabolism runs live under the whole genome: 23 reactions with the published rate constants. The enzymes are the proteins the genome is making right now, so a gene's fate reaches the fuel supply, and the fuel supply reaches every gene.

Energy in

Glucose comes in through a transporter that spends one PEP to import it. Glycolysis splits it, and phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase make ATP. They also make GTP when they grab GDP instead of ADP, the cell's only source of GTP. Some pyruvate becomes lactate, and some goes to acetate through acetate kinase, which makes one more ATP.

Energy out

Ribosomes spend GTP to move along an mRNA and ATP to load each amino acid. ATP synthase, running backwards, burns the spare ATP to pump protons out. It burns less when the ribosomes need more. All of these rates are live in the map below.

How the two talk

Every second of cell time, enzyme levels are read from the live protein counts, and the busy ribosomes set the energy demand. Then the ribosomes' speed is set from ATP and GTP. Gene expression is exact random events and metabolism is equations, the same split the published whole-cell model uses.

Four things to try

Starve it

Drop glucose to almost nothing. For about four minutes nothing seems to happen, because the cell lives off its stockpile of glycolytic intermediates (watch FBP shrink). Then the stockpile runs out, PEP vanishes, glucose import stops, and GTP collapses. The ribosomes stall on GTP while ATP sits unused.

Feed it again

Put the glucose back. Recovery is slow: importing glucose needs PEP, and only glycolysis makes PEP, so the pathway has to rebuild itself from the last few molecules. Expect 10 to 30 minutes of cell time.

Poison GAPDH

A drug that blocks 98% of GAPDH works at once. Everything downstream drains, and gene expression slows across the whole genome.

Knock out pfkA

Deleting the gene is slower than a drug. The enzyme already made only dilutes as the cell grows, halving every 105 minutes. Run at 300× and watch PFK's arrow thin out.

Where the numbers come from

The 23 reactions, their rate constants and affinities, and the starting concentrations are the thermodynamically balanced set published with the whole-cell model (Thornburg et al. 2022). Gene–reaction rules come from the iMB155 reconstruction (Breuer et al. 2019). The rate law is the standard common modular form, and medium glucose is 40 mM, as in their simulations.

What is our choice

How ribosome speed depends on fuel. Ribosomes run at the measured 10 amino acids per second and slow only when GTP or ATP is nearly gone (half speed at 1 µM), as in the published model, where translation draws GTP at full rate until the pool is empty. The cell grows exactly as fast as its ribosomes add amino acids, so a starved cell stops diluting its proteins, as real ones do. Proteins mass spec never detected carry a 10-copy placeholder and aren't allowed to limit an enzyme.

What is still missing

Found in 3d: GTP sits on a knife edge here because this network has no phosphate importer, so glycolysis locks phosphate away in sugar phosphates. Stage 3d adds the importer and the published model's own ATP synthase, and ATP and GTP rise to their published levels. Also missing: the rest of metabolism: amino acids and nucleotides arrive by transport, and lipids are made for the membrane. Above all, growth. Here the ribosome count is fixed, so a starved cell can't shrink its own demand. Stage 3d adds growth and division, so a well-fed cell makes more ribosomes and a starved one makes fewer.
