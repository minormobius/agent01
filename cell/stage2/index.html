Where the enzymes sit

Stage 1 treated the cell as a well-mixed bag. Here every metabolite is a particle taking real Brownian steps through a 400 nm cell. Both cells have the same 60 + 60 enzymes and the same rate constants. Only the arrangement differs: scattered on the left, assembled into clusters on the right. A well-mixed model predicts identical yields for both.

What is simulated

Brownian dynamics with Doi-model reactions. Metabolites diffuse at 50 µm²/s and enzymes at 2 µm²/s, in 0.25 µs steps. A molecule within 6 nm of a free enzyme is captured with probability ½ per step. The enzyme then stays busy for an exponential time averaging 15 µs. Substrate enters through 12 membrane transporters. Product leaves at the membrane, and so does the intermediate, with the chance set above.

What is simplified

The cell is a 2D slice, so diffusion is planar and capture rates are not those of a real 3D cytoplasm. There is no crowding, no explicit enzyme–substrate complex, and no regulation. The parameters are physically plausible but not fitted to any organism. Notice the cost of clustering too: S builds up because there are fewer places to capture it.
