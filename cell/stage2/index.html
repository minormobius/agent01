Stage 2 · spatial reaction–diffusion

Where the enzymes sit

Stage 1 counted molecules but ignored where they were. Here every small molecule is a particle bouncing randomly through the cell, and an enzyme can only act on one that drifts within 6 nm of it. That makes position matter. Below, two cells with exactly the same enzymes differ only in where those enzymes sit, and they make different amounts of product.

The pathway in both cells

The catch

I is leaky: every time it bumps into the membrane, it has a chance of slipping out and being lost. The longer I wanders before E2 catches it, the more of it escapes.

The score

Yield is the share of I that E2 catches and turns into P, rather than losing it. 100% would mean nothing escapes.

The question

If E1 and E2 are packed side by side, does I get caught sooner? A model that only counts molecules says it makes no difference. Watch the two cells and see.

What every enzyme is doing

Each row is one enzyme, 60 E1 rows above 60 E2 rows. A bar means that enzyme is busy converting a molecule, which takes about 15 µs. Blank means it is waiting for something to drift close enough. Compare the E2 rows: in the clustered cell, E2 is busier, because I is made right next to it and gets caught before it can reach the membrane.

Three things to try

Seal the membrane

Set leakiness to 0. Nothing can escape, so both yields go to 100%. Arrangement only matters because I is leaky.

One giant cluster

Put all 120 enzymes in one cluster. I is caught almost instantly, but S now has to find one spot in the whole cell. Watch the S pool grow.

A very leaky intermediate

Raise leakiness to 100%: any I that touches the membrane is lost. The scattered cell suffers most, and the gap between the two cells widens.

What is simulated

Brownian dynamics with Doi-model reactions. Small molecules diffuse at 50 µm²/s and enzymes at 2 µm²/s, in 0.25 µs steps. A molecule within 6 nm of a free enzyme is caught with probability ½ per step. The enzyme then stays busy for a random time averaging 15 µs. S enters through 12 transporters.

What is simplified

The cell is a 2D slice, so diffusion is flat and capture rates are not those of a real 3D cytoplasm. There is no crowding and no regulation, and the parameters are physically plausible but not fitted to any organism. Clustering has a cost too: S piles up, because there are fewer places to catch it.
