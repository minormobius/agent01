morph · a city's form

A town plan is three things laid down at different times: the streets, the plots, and the buildings on the plots (M. R. G. Conzen, 1960). Each district here was laid out in one era by one idea of a street: an organic core, a planned grid, a boulevard scheme round a rond-point, a modern grid, a suburb. Each lattice runs at its own angle, so where two meet the blocks are cut short: a boulevard on the seam, and triangular plots where flatirons go.

Every block is shared among the streets it fronts (the land nearest each street is its), and each frontage is cut into plots running back to the block's middle: narrow burgage plots in the old core, wider and often merged ones later. Buildings stand on the front of their plots, as deep, tall and roofed as their era built. Every cut is a convex clip, so the plots tile the blocks exactly. Plan: drag to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, tap to inspect. Model: drag to turn, shift-drag or two fingers to pan.
