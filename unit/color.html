tap one to jump there

How the colors are made. Wavelength → CIE 1931 2° standard observer → XYZ → sRGB. Every pure spectral color lies outside the sRGB gamut, so no screen can show one: the swatches here are the closest it gets, and the meter above says how close. The strip is drawn saturated, the way a spectrum poster is; the “true” chip in the readouts is the desaturated color that keeps the dominant wavelength exact.

Why the reverse only half works. A wavelength has exactly one color. A color does not have one wavelength — greys have none, magentas have none, and every other color names only the pure light it leans toward. Color-matching data: CIE 1931 2° observer at 5 nm, linearly interpolated.
