Thomas of Erceldoune

The romance of Thomas the Rhymer: a May morning under the Eildon Hills. A lady of another country on a dapple-grey horse, a beauty spent at a touch, three days of darkness and water under the hill, fruit that must not be eaten, five roads, a hall where he may speak to no one, three days that were three years, the teind to hell, and a tongue that can never lie. The sixth tale in Samhain, the series.

A deep read of the Middle English romance. The source is J. A. H. Murray's edition of all five manuscripts, The Romance and Prophecies of Thomas of Erceldoune, EETS o.s. 61 (1875), following the Thornton MS (Lincoln Cathedral 91, c. 1430–40) with its gaps filled from Cambridge Ff. 5. 48 and Lansdowne 762 — public domain, transcribed from the Internet Archive scan, with the raw OCR at source/. Fytte I is complete; the prophecies of Fyttes II–III are abridged, and each cut is marked. The English is an original translation. This is the sixth tale of the Samhain series, and its first from Scotland; the six are compared on the series hub. Its descendant, Tam Lin, is on the roster.
