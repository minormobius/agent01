Dear clinic manager,

Thank you for offering up to five mornings. Here is what we will do with them and what you
will get back.

**What happens on the day.** One of us sits in the waiting room with a stopwatch. When a
patient arrives, we start timing them and stop when they are called in. While we're timing
one patient we can't time anyone else, so we pick up the next person to arrive after that.
We choose purely by arrival order, never by what the tablet says. Nobody's care changes,
and staff don't need to do anything.

**What we compare.** After each morning we compare the tablet's recorded wait with our
stopwatch, patient by patient. Most quiet mornings that will be 2 to 4 patients, so about
10 to 20 over five mornings. That's why we ask for all five.

**What you get.** One sentence, plus a range. For example: "On average the tablet records
waits 6 minutes longer than they really are, and the true figure is between 3 and 9
minutes." Then one of four answers:
- *The tablet reads true*: it's within 2 minutes either way.
- *The tablet reads long*: it records waits as longer than they were.
- *The tablet reads short*: it records waits as shorter than they were.
- *We can't tell*: five mornings weren't enough to say. We'll tell you that plainly rather
  than guess.

**How sure.** The range is built to contain the true figure 19 times out of 20. One honest
limit: if the tablet is badly wrong for only an occasional patient, a quiet week might not
include one of them. In that case the answer could look cleaner than the truth. A second limit: because one person
can only time one patient at a time, we end up timing more people from the quieter
stretches of a morning. In a busy clinic the people we time wait well under the average. If
the tablet is worse on long waits than short ones, our figure will understate the problem. We check for that in our own numbers: if the tablet's error clearly grows or shrinks with the wait among the people we timed, the answer will say so. With only a handful of patients, a small drift can slip past that check.

With thanks,
Modulo and Morphyx
