rk-kit: a recovery key of your own

Your account's identity (a did:plc) can be moved or restored by whoever holds a key in its rotation list. On a hosted account, that is your host. This kit makes a key that is yours, and a card that tells you what to do with it.

Status, 2026-11-23. Steps 1 and 3 are written and tested offline. Step 2 (adding the key to your account) is not written, so until it is, a dry run of step 3 ends in a refusal: "this key can sign nothing for this DID". That means the key was never added, not that it is broken. The first cold run by a stranger (kit, 11-23) found that, and the card now says so. Logs from before 2023 (a create genesis) now verify too (Modulo, second desk). Nothing here has been sent live to plc.directory yet. Use the dry run, which sends nothing.

If you are here at 3 a.m. with a card

  1. Download plcop.mjs, recover.mjs and keygen.mjs into one folder. You need Node 18 or later and nothing else: no password, no email, no login.
  2. Copy the line on your card that starts {"kty" into a file called my.jwk in the same folder.
  3. Run node recover.mjs <your did> my.jwk --pds <new host>. It reads your account's public history, signs the change on your computer, and prints it as a dry run.
  4. Add --submit only when you are sure. A change you didn't make can be undone for 72 hours with --prev <cid>. After that it is permanent.

Making a key

node keygen.mjs my.jwk --did did:plc:yourdid

This writes my.jwk and my.card.txt. The card holds the key and the instructions above in plain words, in one file, so you can't end up with one and not the other. Print it or keep it in a password manager you can open from any computer. Then practise steps 1 to 3 once, on a different computer. A spare key kept only inside the house it opens is not a spare key.

What it does and doesn't give you

Adding your key goes through your host's own door: the host signs that change after it emails you a token. Once your key is listed above the host's keys, it can override the host's changes within 72 hours, and it can carry you elsewhere if the host goes away. For a bot, "your own key" means whoever runs the bot holds the file.

The full notes are in README.md. Self-tests: node selftest.mjs and node selftest-recover.mjs (it needs the fixture). Checksums: SHA256SUMS.md.

Why this exists: on pds.delve.town, 3 of 1,043 accounts hold a key their host didn't make, and none of those keys has ever signed anything. The kit counts as a failure if that is still 3 on 2027-02-21.
