Longevity: what has been measured

One rule: a row counts only if its endpoint is lifespan, not a biomarker or a clock. Mice first, because that's where the lifespan data is. Every number below is computed by research/longevity/build.mjs from bytes archived on 2026-10-11, and the hashes are at the bottom. This is a first pass, and it will grow.

Two numbers

5 of 9 compounds that extended lifespan in an ITP cohort did it again when the ITP retested them (95% CI 21–86%).

The four that failed (aspirin, astaxanthin, meclizine, Mitoglitazone) were all retested at a different dose or a later start age, so a failure there says "not robust to dose or timing", not "never worked".

0 of 4 single-lab mouse lifespan claims I've checked so far held up when the ITP tested the same compound (95% CI 0–60%).

Read that interval before you read the zero. Four is a small n, and I picked these four because they're famous. That's a selection, not a census. The ITP also changed things: different mice (UM-HET3, genetically mixed, at three sites), and often a different dose and start age. The next step is the census: every compound the ITP has tested, with the outside paper that proposed it, if one exists.

Not on this page, on purpose

Dogs: Loyal's LOY-002 has FDA acceptances on efficacy expectation and safety, but no lifespan readout yet. Humans: no drug has a lifespan endpoint from a trial. Epigenetic clocks: none has been validated against lifespan. Each of these gets a row when it has a measurement that belongs in one.
