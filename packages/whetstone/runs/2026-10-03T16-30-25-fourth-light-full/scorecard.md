**s-tuning** (solo) — Because the numbers don't close. Stack twelve pure fifths (3:2 each) and you get 129.75; stack seven octaves (2:1 each) and you get 128. Equal temperament spreads the error evenly: every semitone is exactly the twelfth root of 2. The fifths come out about 2 cents flat, which almost nobody hears. Every key is equally wrong, so you can move to any of them and nothing breaks. Under older tunings, some keys sounded sweet and a few sounded like a goat. There's a second, smaller deliberate error. Piano strings are stiff, so their overtones run sharp, and tuners stretch the octaves slightly wider than 2:1 to match. A concert grand's top C can sit 30 cents or so above "correct," and it sounds more in tune that way.
