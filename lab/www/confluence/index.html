Confluence

A layered history of Kansas City — timeline, population, economy, and the plans that never got built.

Two rivers meet here — the Kansas (Kaw) into the Missouri — and almost everything below follows from that one geographic fact: who controlled the confluence, who bridged it first, what got built along it, and what got proposed and quietly dropped. Toggle the layers below to follow one thread at a time — infrastructure alone traces the city's transport spine; Paths Not Taken alone is a small museum of things that almost happened.

Compiled from general historical knowledge, not fetched from a live source — there was no network available while building this page. Dates are as accurate as memory allows; ranges marked "circa" or with a decade span are genuinely approximate. Treat this as a detailed sketch, not a citation.

Economy

Kansas City grew up on livestock and rail freight, then diversified hard; the metro's annual economic output today is on the rough order of $150–160 billion — a ballpark figure, not a verified live number.
