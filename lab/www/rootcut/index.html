rootcut

Mark a root set on a graph. Then either pack in vertex-disjoint paths between roots, or prove you can't with a small cut.

This is Menger's theorem, playable. For two chosen vertices, the maximum number of vertex-disjoint paths between them always equals the minimum number of vertices whose removal disconnects them — packing and cutting are the same number, always. Gallai's theorem extends this from a single pair to a whole root set S: pack disjoint paths between any pairs of roots, or find a small set of vertices that kills every possible one. This toy plays the S-path case (the "T = K2" case of the paper this site is built from) — build a graph, mark roots, and try both roles yourself.

One simplification, said plainly: this toy requires packed paths to be completely vertex-disjoint, including their endpoints. The classical theorem is slightly more generous — it lets two S-paths share an endpoint in S — which is more paths to verify duality against and out of scope for a first pass. Weak duality still holds exactly as shown below: any valid cover needs at least as many vertices as your packing, since it has to hit each of your disjoint paths at least once.
