Guard the Cage

A dominating set is a set of vertices where every other vertex is next door to one of them. The domination number is the smallest such set. Below is a truncated octahedron — 24 corners, every one touching exactly 3 edges — the exact solid a 2026 paper used to break a 1998 conjecture about how many edges you'd have to cut to force that number up. Click corners to guard them; every other corner lights up green the moment a guard is standing next to it. The paper's own minimum for this shape is 8. Can you find it?

Construction: the 24 corners are every permutation of (0, ±1, ±2); two corners share an edge exactly when they're distance √2 apart, which gives every corner exactly 3 neighbours and 36 edges total — the standard coordinates for a truncated octahedron. Domination here means a corner is "covered" if it is itself guarded or directly wired to a guarded corner. This build only plays the domination side of the paper's result: it does not yet let you cut edges to hunt for a smaller bondage number (the paper's actual new record — 5 edges, against a conjectured ceiling of 4) — that's the next turn. Built with three.js, runs entirely in your browser: nothing you click here is sent anywhere.
