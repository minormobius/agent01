Chess, Except

You still pick the piece. The piece picks the moves — a whole random walk of them, one legal step after another, until it's got nowhere left to go.

the except part

  • Tap a piece with a legal move. It moves — then, from wherever it lands, moves again, and again, choosing uniformly at random among its own legal moves each time, until it has none left. Then it's the other side's turn.
  • A walk can chain captures: land on an enemy piece, take it, and keep walking from that square if there's still somewhere legal to go.
  • No pawn promotion — a pawn that reaches the back rank is just a pawn that reached the back rank. It often has no legal move left there, which ends the walk on its own.
  • No castling, no en passant, no check or checkmate — this variant doesn't track attack squares.
  • Kings can't attack anything. A king only ever steps onto an empty square — it can walk right up next to an enemy piece, even the enemy king, but it never captures.
  • Kings get 3 lives: the first two pieces that land a capture on a king are destroyed instead of completing it, and the king loses one life each time. The third hit actually takes the king and ends the game.
  • Every other piece except pawns (knight, bishop, rook, queen) gets 1 extra life too, shown as a small dot on the piece. The first piece to capture it is destroyed instead and the dot disappears; the second capture actually lands.
  • The board is 8 wide and 12 tall, with two ranks of pawns on each side instead of one — the back rank's pawns are boxed in until the front rank moves.
  • A walk has no step limit — it runs until the piece has nowhere legal left to go, and steps play out quickly. New game abandons the current one at any point.
  • The game also ends if a side has no legal move anywhere on its turn (a draw).
