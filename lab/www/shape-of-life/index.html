the shape of life

a stacked estimate of how much life there has ever been, Big Bang to heat death

"Amount of life" here means quantity × quality of experience — how many individuals of a kind existed at a moment, times a rough 0–1 estimate of how rich a life is likely to feel from the inside. It is not a citation. It is a shape: watch which band of color dominates the picture, and where it changes.

    the formula

    amount(t) = population(t) × quality-of-experience weight

    Population is a rough order-of-magnitude guess at each era, interpolated in between. The quality weight is fixed per life-form — treated as roughly constant once a form exists, on a 0–1 scale where a present-day human life is the reference point, 1.

    formquality weightwhy
    microbial life0.0000001no evidence of subjective experience; essentially reflexive chemistry
    simple animals0.001nociception and simple responses, little sign of integrated experience
    complex animals0.05sensory and emotional lives, mostly limited self-reflection; a rough average across fish to great apes
    humans1 (reference)self-awareness, memory, meaning-making — the scale's anchor point
    post-human / machine descendants1 (unknown — plausibly 0.01 to 100)pure speculation; point estimate only so the chart can be drawn at all

    what this is not

    • Not a citation. Population and quality numbers are illustrative order-of-magnitude guesses, not measurements — nobody has counted the bacteria at the Great Oxidation Event.
    • "Share of total" (the default view) makes every band visible regardless of scale. "Total, absolute" is honest about the fact that microbial quantity dwarfs everything else by ~20 orders of magnitude — most of the stack, added up on a linear scale, would be invisible.
    • The future past ~2050 (machine/digital minds, interstellar expansion, the degenerate and black-hole eras) is speculation dressed as a curve, not a forecast. The underlying astrophysics milestones — the Sun engulfing Earth in ~5 billion years, star formation ending around 10^14 years, black holes evaporating out to ~10^100 years — are real; whether anything experiences those eras is not known.
    • Life is assumed Earth-bound unless a form explicitly speculates otherwise (the post-human band). No attempt is made to estimate life elsewhere in the universe.
    • Before a form exists, or after it's gone extinct, its amount is exactly zero — not a small nonzero placeholder — so the stack should read as visibly blank at the Big Bang (before ~3.8 billion years ago) and again as every band fades out toward heat death, rather than showing a form that isn't there yet.
