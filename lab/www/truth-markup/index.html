Truth Markup

what a price does once the lies stop covering for it

Five things people move around all day — DRAM, NAND, HBM, transfer bandwidth, supply capacity — are all priced a little low, because some of what everyone believes about them is wrong. Dispel a lie below. The suppressed price rises toward what the thing actually costs, and whatever misinformation you removed doesn't vanish — it gets kept, shrunk down to the tiniest piece of art, in the gallery underneath. Each dispel sends a shuriken cutting across the chart — the lie actually getting cut, not just a number moving. Prices don't stop rising once they catch up to "true," either — the more of this gets dispelled, the more the market actually wants the honest number, so demand for truth keeps climbing past it rather than settling once supply catches up.

    All five lines share one price axis, drawn on a log scale — NAND moves in cents and HBM moves in tens of dollars, and without that the cheap line would look flat at the bottom no matter how much noise it actually had.

      This is a small illustrative simulation, not a live price feed — the "true values" are approximate, honestly-labelled stand-ins for real DRAM/NAND/HBM economics, not sourced from any market data. "True value" is a floor here, not a ceiling: once purity is high enough, demand for the honest number pushes prices above it, deliberately inverted from the usual rule that more supply cools price. The price simulation itself always resets on refresh. Sign in above and the gallery — only the gallery — saves to your own Bluesky repo, so it survives; signed out, it's gone when you leave. "Save gallery as one image" works either way, signed in or not — it composites whatever tiles are on screen into a single PNG you can download. "Animate the gallery" is the moving version of the same idea — it crossfades through the same tiles in your browser; there's no file to save from it, since building a GIF or video needs an encoder this page has no way to fetch. Racing a rival needs you signed in to post your own score; looking someone else's up never does — that just reads their public repo. Signed in status itself is meant to persist across visits (up to 30 days, kept alive automatically while you're active) — if it isn't sticking for you, that's most likely the browser or app you're viewing this in clearing its storage between visits, not something toggled per-visit here.
