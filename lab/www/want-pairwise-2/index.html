how this is computed

For each handle we read the most recent public activity (up to 100 posts from getAuthorFeed) and count who it targets: the author of a post it replied to, the author of a post it reposted, and the author of a post it quoted. Original posts with no reply/repost/quote target don't point at anyone and aren't counted. Each target's tally is its interaction weight; the highest-weight accounts are that handle's "circle."

Take the union of both circles as a lattice of sites. At each site k we have two numbers: how much handle A engages it (ak) and how much handle B does (bk). The Pearson correlation of those two vectors across the lattice is the two-point correlation function C — do the accounts that pull on A also pull on B, and by how much.

For two Ising spins with no external field, ⟨s₁s₂⟩ = tanh(βJ). We invert that: J = atanh(C), in units where kT = 1. J > 0 reads as ferromagnetic (their circles reinforce each other); J < 0 as antiferromagnetic (engaging one circle pulls against the other).

Susceptibility follows the Curie–Weiss mean-field law χ = 1 / (1 − J), which diverges at the critical coupling J = 1 (C ≈ 0.762). Past that point mean-field theory itself stops being valid, so instead of printing a number we say the pair is past critical — treat it as "locked," not as a magnitude.

This is a physics-flavored toy, not a measurement. A hundred recent posts is a small, recency-biased sample; a real two-point correlation function needs many more sites and a real Hamiltonian to fit. Read C, J and χ as a playful lens on public engagement patterns, not as a claim about anyone's actual social ties.
