the pay cliff

a histogram of who actually gets paid to do this

A thread about which careers are safe from AI landed, half-joking, on competitive sports — the reasoning being something like "not enough humans can do that at all, AI or not." That's closer to true than the AI part of the argument. Below are six sports, each as a histogram of roughly where the people seriously chasing it — tour cards, minor-league contracts, national squads, not weekend hobbyists — land on estimated lifetime earnings. The dashed line marks a rough "full-time" threshold. Drag it and watch how few bars clear it in some of these, before AI ever enters the picture.

Rough stand-in for what a multi-year career needs to clear, after training and travel costs, to beat working a normal job instead of being subsidized by one. Not a researched figure — move it and judge for yourself.

What this is (and isn't)

This build has no network access and no real per-athlete earnings dataset to query. Every bar here is a hand-built approximation shaped to match well-known, publicly reported facts about each sport's pay cliff — league minimums, tour-card cutoffs, stipend levels, the gap between headline star money and everyone else — not a measurement of real individuals. Treat the shapes as roughly right and the exact percentages as illustrative, not cited figures.

The population modeled for each sport is people competing at a serious, competitive level (tour rankings, minor-league or reserve contracts, national training squads) — not casual participants, and not a literal headcount. Lifetime earnings are on-field/on-court prize money and salary only in most bins; a handful of sports also fold in the endorsement money that drives their extreme top tail.

The "clears the line" percentage for a bin straddling the threshold is interpolated assuming earnings are spread evenly across that bin on a log scale — a simplification, not a real income curve.

Estimates only — see "How these were estimated" above before citing anything here as fact.
