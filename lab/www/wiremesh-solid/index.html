Wiremesh ⇄ Solid

A cluster built from smaller copies of itself: each node carries a group of children arranged the same way it is arranged around its own parent, all the way down. Every node also oscillates by the same rule as its parent — smaller, faster, out of phase — so the jitter of the whole is made of the same jitter repeated at every scale.

Drag the viewport to look around. Floating mode auto-rotates the whole cluster and drag adds to it; fly-through mode freezes the cluster in place and drag steers the camera — use the on-screen thumbsticks (or W/S) to move through it.

Construction: start from one point, attach four children toward the corners of a tetrahedron at half the parent's scale, then repeat on each child up to the chosen depth — the same recursive placement a Sierpinski tetrahedron uses. Every node's position each frame is its parent's current position plus its own offset, scaled by sin(time · frequency + phase) where frequency and amplitude shrink by the same factor as the geometry does per level — so a small piece of the structure, watched alone, moves exactly like the whole thing does. Built with three.js, runs entirely in your browser: nothing here is sent anywhere. Honours "reduce motion" by holding the cluster still and turning off auto-rotate.
