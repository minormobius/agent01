Newman, Borwein & Littlewood polynomial roots

Five families of low-complexity polynomials, plotted by every complex root of every one of them: Newman polynomials (coefficients in {0,1}) up to degree 20, Borwein polynomials (coefficients in {−1,0,1}) up to degree 12, imaginary Littlewood polynomials (coefficients in {i,−i}) up to degree 20, cyclotomic Littlewood polynomials (coefficients in {−1,1,i,−i}) up to degree 10, and cube-root polynomials (coefficients drawn from the three cube roots of unity {1,ω,ω²}) up to degree 12 — computed right here in your browser.

 

One black pixel per root on a white field — darker patches are just many roots landing on the same pixel. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom.

A Newman polynomial has every coefficient equal to 0 or 1, with the leading and constant terms fixed at 1 — that gives 2d−1 of them at degree d. Named for Donald J. Newman, their roots pile up densely in an annulus roughly between |z| = 1/φ and |z| = φ (the golden ratio, φ ≈ 1.618): a 0/1 polynomial with constant term 1 can never have a root much closer to the origin or much further out.

A Borwein polynomial widens that to {−1, 0, 1}, still fixing the leading and constant terms at 1, which gives 3d−1 of them at degree d — enough more that the ceiling here is lower (degree 12, versus 20 for Newman) to keep the run in the same ballpark of time. Their roots are only guaranteed to stay under |z| = 2 (Cauchy's general bound for a monic polynomial with coefficients no larger than 1) — a looser fit than Newman's golden-ratio ring, so expect a broader, fuzzier spread rather than a crisp annulus.

An imaginary Littlewood polynomial here takes every coefficient from {i,−i} instead of a real alphabet, still with leading and constant terms fixed at i — 2d−1 of them at degree d, same count as Newman. Multiplying every coefficient of a polynomial by the nonzero constant i can't move a root (p(z) = 0 exactly when i·p(z) = 0), so this family's roots are computed as the real polynomial with the same {−1,1} pattern — the classic Littlewood alphabet, not otherwise built here — which is exactly where the imaginary version's roots land too. It's the family as asked for — the shortcut used to compute it is an exact equivalence, not an approximation.

A cyclotomic Littlewood polynomial is the genuinely complex family: every interior coefficient is independently one of the four 4th roots of unity {−1, 1, i, −i} (the roots of z4 = 1, hence “cyclotomic”), with leading and constant terms fixed at 1 — 4d−1 of them at degree d, which is why the ceiling here is lower still (degree 10). Unlike imaginary Littlewood, this one can't be collapsed back to a real family by multiplying through by a single constant — a given polynomial mixes real and imaginary coefficients, so no one scalar rotates all of them onto the real axis at once. The root finder carries genuinely complex coefficients for this family; fixing leading and constant at real 1 keeps every polynomial here exactly monic, which is what keeps that solver correct without further changes.

A cube-root polynomial draws every interior coefficient independently from the three cube roots of unity {1, ω, ω²} (the solutions of z3 = 1, where ω = e2πi/3), with leading and constant terms fixed at real 1 — 3d−1 of them at degree d, the same count as Borwein, so it shares Borwein's degree-12 ceiling. Like cyclotomic Littlewood it's genuinely complex through the solver (real Durand-Kerner doesn't apply), and for the same reason fixing the ends at 1 keeps every polynomial exactly monic so the solver needs no further changes.

Roots are found numerically with the Durand–Kerner (Weierstrass) method, run to convergence or a fixed iteration cap per polynomial — they are close approximations, not exact algebraic values, and a handful of nearly-repeated roots at high degree may be slightly off. The full run is exponential in the degree, so it runs in small chunks between frames rather than blocking the page; the progress bar tracks it, and Stop keeps whatever has plotted so far.

Everything above runs locally: no server, no stored data, nothing sent anywhere.
