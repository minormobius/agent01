Newman polynomial roots

A Newman polynomial is one whose coefficients are all 0 or 1 — leading coefficient 1, constant term 1, everything in between free. There are 2d−1 of them at degree d. This plots every complex root of every one of them, for every degree from 1 up to 15, computed right here in your browser.

Colour marks the average degree contributing to a pixel; brightness marks how many roots land there — drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom.

Named for Donald J. Newman, who studied polynomials restricted to these coefficients. As the degree grows, their roots don't scatter randomly — they pile up densely in an annulus roughly between |z| = 1/φ and |z| = φ (the golden ratio, φ ≈ 1.618), since a 0/1-coefficient polynomial with constant term 1 can never have a root much closer to the origin or much further out. Watch the plot fill in as you push the degree up: individual roots at low degree give way to a dense, textured band at degree 15.

Roots are found numerically with the Durand–Kerner (Weierstrass) method, run to convergence or a fixed iteration cap per polynomial — they are close approximations, not exact algebraic values, and a handful of nearly-repeated roots at high degree may be slightly off. The full run is exponential in the degree — degree 15 alone is 16,384 polynomials — so it runs in small chunks between frames rather than blocking the page; the progress bar tracks it, and Stop keeps whatever has plotted so far.

Everything above runs locally: no server, no stored data, nothing sent anywhere.
