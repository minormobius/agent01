how this is computed, honestly

We resolve your handle, read up to 100 accounts you follow and up to 100 accounts that follow you, and intersect them for your mutuals — up to 8 of them, whichever the API hands back first. For you and each mutual we read up to 100 recent posts (skipping reposts and reply parents that aren't their own words), tokenize the text, drop a short stopword list, and build a relative-frequency vector normalized to unit length. That vector is the "embedding" here — plain bag-of-words, not a trained model. There is no ML embeddings API this environment can reach without a key, so this is the honest substitute, not a pretend one.

Let v be your vector and u₁…uₙ your mutuals' vectors. We solve min ‖v − Σwᵢuᵢ‖² subject to wᵢ ≥ 0 — non-negative least squares — with a small active-set method: solve the unconstrained system, drop the most negative coefficient, re-solve, repeat until every remaining weight is non-negative. That's a simplified NNLS, not the full Lawson–Hanson algorithm, but exact for the handful of mutuals involved here.

The donut's colored slices are each mutual's share of the fit; the grey slice is what the fit couldn't explain. Cosine fit and residual are two independent checks on the same answer, not one number dressed up as two — a high cosine similarity can still hide a lopsided reconstruction, so both are shown rather than asserting "solved" from either alone.

This deliberately shows a blend across several mutuals, not a single closest match. A "which one account does this sound most like" tool is one short step from a target list; a linear combination across everyone in your circle isn't, and that's the version worth building.

Real limitations, stated plainly: only the first page (~100) of your follows and followers is read, so mutuals beyond that aren't seen. Bag-of-words has no sense of meaning, sarcasm, or language beyond English-ish tokenizing — a heavily non-English account will look thin here. Small accounts with little recent text won't have enough signal to fit against.
