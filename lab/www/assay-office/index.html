The Assay Office

Paste text below, or point it at a Bluesky handle's recent posts, and every word that shows up more than once gets weighed and ranked — most-repeated first, down to the words that barely qualify as duplicates at all. It's a naive word tokenizer wearing an assayer's coat: no stemming, no dictionary, "cat" and "cats" are different atoms. Nothing you paste leaves your browser unless you choose the Bluesky option, which only ever reads the handle you typed.

How the grading works

Every run of letters between non-letters is one "atom," lowercased. Only atoms that occur two or more times in the text count as dupes — singletons never make the ledger, because a word that shows up once isn't a duplicate of anything. Above that floor, three grades: bullion (8+ copies), sterling (4–7), alloy (2–3) — best to worst, by raw count, ties broken alphabetically.
