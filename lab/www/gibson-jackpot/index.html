What this actually is

A toy model, not a forecast and not medical or financial advice. The aging part is a Gompertz–Makeham hazard, the standard shape actuaries use for adult mortality. Country risk, state resilience and standing each contribute a relative-risk multiplier — the same log-linear structure a real Cox proportional-hazards model uses — and the three multiply together. Country risk and resilience scores are illustrative estimates written by hand for this build, not sourced statistics. The "jackpot" is a single global scenario, the same for everyone: population decline follows a normalized Weibull cumulative distribution — with x = clip(t − 2039, 0, 40), D(t) = 0.8 · (1 − e−(x⁄24.3)2.2) ⁄ (1 − e−(40⁄24.3)2.2), and P(t) = P₀ · (1 − D(t)), with P₀ fixed at 2026's population. It sits flat until 2039, ramps through the 2040s–70s, and lands at 20% of 2026's population by around 2079, holding there through 2099 — a smooth stand-in for "not a war, not a single event, a slow-motion accounting," not a prediction of how it would actually happen. The per-person hazard layered onto your own curve above is the instantaneous rate implied by that same D(t), so the two charts agree exactly rather than approximately.
