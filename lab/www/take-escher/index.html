Shoal

A real hyperbolic tiling, not just curved rows: tessellating polygons generated from a triangle-group reflection, each carrying a fish silhouette warped to fit it, mapped into the Poincaré disk. Drag anywhere and the whole shoal glides along that geodesic — every fish stays locked to its neighbours automatically, because it's one rigid conformal move applied to the whole tiling, not many independent ones. New tiles grow in continuously just ahead of where you're swimming, and old ones quietly retire once they've panned out near the rim — so it never runs out, and refilling never shows as a jump or a stall.

Drag inside the disk to pan the whole tiling along that direction. The swim buttons repeat the last direction you used.

Tiles here are the actual polygons of a hyperbolic {p,q} tiling — built by reflecting a fundamental triangle across its own edges, so adjacent tiles share exact edges and there are no gaps. They correctly shrink toward the rim by the Poincaré conformal factor. Only a buffer of tiles around the current view is kept alive at once: a few new ones grow in every frame near wherever you're panning, and ones that have drifted out near the rim are retired to make room, instead of throwing the whole visible patch away and rebuilding it in one jump. Each tile now carries a fish silhouette warped to fit it, alternating direction by BFS-depth parity (a stand-in for a true two-coloring). The underlying lines are still geodesics rather than the equidistant curves the real woodcut uses — see BRIEF.md for both.
