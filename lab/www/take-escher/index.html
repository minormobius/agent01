Shoal

A real hyperbolic tiling, not just curved rows: tessellating polygons generated from a triangle-group reflection, mapped into the Poincaré disk. Drag anywhere and the whole pattern glides along that geodesic — every tile stays locked to its neighbours automatically, because it's one rigid conformal move applied to the whole tiling, not many independent ones.

Drag inside the disk to pan the whole tiling along that direction. The swim buttons repeat the last direction you used.

Tiles here are the actual polygons of a hyperbolic {p,q} tiling — built by reflecting a fundamental triangle across its own edges, so adjacent tiles share exact edges and there are no gaps. They correctly shrink toward the rim by the Poincaré conformal factor. They're plain tinted polygons, not Escher's fish silhouette yet, and the underlying lines are geodesics rather than the equidistant curves the real woodcut uses — see BRIEF.md for both.
