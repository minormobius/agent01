Shepherd

An unseen planet stirs a ring of dust as it passes through the disk. Its closest pass kicks dust hard enough to catch the stellar wind and race outward; its farthest pass, on the opposite side, barely stirs anything.

what this is

A 2026 paper models the real debris disk around the star AU Microscopii, whose images show dust clumps that race outward above the disk's mid-plane on one side, and drift near it on the other — with no confirmed cause. The paper's mechanism: an unconfirmed planet, on an eccentric orbit tilted relative to the disk, punches through the disk plane twice per orbit. Stellar wind only pushes a dust grain outward once a kick has lofted it above a threshold height. Their best match to the images used a roughly Jupiter-mass planet a few AU out, on a fairly eccentric, moderately tilted orbit.

This toy is a simplified, honest caricature of that idea, not a reproduction of the real images or the paper's own numbers. Because the planet's closest approach to the star (periapsis) is where the disk-crossing happens to land in this model, that crossing kicks hard; the far crossing (apoapsis), on the opposite side of the disk, is much gentler — that asymmetry is exactly what the eccentricity slider controls. Push it to zero and both sides kick equally; push it up and one side does almost all the work, same as the paper's finding.

The wind threshold is the other half of the trade-off: set it low and almost every kicked grain gets swept outward, emptying that side of the ring quickly; set it high and even a strong kick fades back into the plane before the wind ever catches it.

what isn't real physics here

No self-gravity, no real radiation-pressure dynamics, no attempt to match AU Mic's actual clump speeds or the paper's own five numbers. The kick and wind strengths, the "spring" that settles a kicked grain back toward the plane, and the recycling of grains that wander too far out are all invented constants tuned by eye, not fit to data — untested in a live browser before this shipped, so they may want retuning.
