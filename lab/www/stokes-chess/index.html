Stokes Chess

This is a real fluid solver first: a 48×48 grid running Jos Stam's stable-fluids method — semi-Lagrangian advection, a pressure projection so the flow stays (nearly) incompressible, and viscosity switched off entirely, so nothing damps a current except the solver's own numerical decay. Flow strength defaults to a violent 100×. Six fixed vents around the centre — alternating pump and drain, each wobbling on its own slow schedule — keep pumping fluid in and draining it out permanently, so the board is never still: every cell carries a strong current at all times, the way water never actually stops moving in a storm. Drag anywhere to stir on top of that and watch your current join the ambient one and decay back into it. The chess set stands in the middle of all this: each piece behaves like a micron-scale object in a very viscous fluid — no coasting, no inertia, it just gets carried while the local current pushes and stops the instant it doesn't — so pieces sway constantly, like ships riding a swell, and drift a square when the current under them is strong enough. Moving a piece also releases its own current scaled to how far it travelled. The piece that just moved is immune to its own wake and always lands dead-centre on its target; every other occupied square samples the live velocity field each frame, sways, and slides into the next square if the drag wins — blocked, not captured, if that square is occupied. Movement shapes only: no check, checkmate, castling or en passant, and pawns auto-promote to a queen.

White to move
Each occupied square keeps a small offset (ox, oy) from its square's centre. Every frame it relaxes toward the locally sampled velocity v instead of accelerating toward it — there is no momentum term, which is the correct model for a very small object in a viscous fluid (low Reynolds number / Stokes drag: viscous forces dominate inertia, so a particle's speed tracks the local flow directly rather than building up over time): offset = RESTORE · offset + RESTORE · DRAG · v / mass Heavier pieces (queen, king) divide DRAG by a larger mass and resist more. When |offset| on the dominant axis crosses a threshold, that piece's board position actually changes — a real move, not a wobble — and its offset resets to zero. The arrows drawn over the board are a live vector field, one direct sample per grid point, not a traced or sketched line. Six fixed points around the board's centre call addVelocity/addDensity every frame — alternating pump (positive) and drain (density clamped at zero, so it empties rather than going negative) — each wobbling in strength and angle on its own slow, irregular sine schedule. That is the permanent storm; a drag adds a second, temporary current on top of it.

Approximate on purpose: 4 Jacobi iterations per projection (not a true divergence-free solve, cheap enough to run every frame on a phone), and the flow-strength/storm numbers were reasoned from the relaxation's settle-time math rather than measured in a browser — this build has neither. Viscosity is fixed at zero, so nothing in the solver itself damps the current; only its own numerical decay and the piece drag do. Move the slider if a push (or the permanent storm) feels instant or inert; that is what it is for. Nothing here is saved between visits.
