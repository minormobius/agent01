Enter two Bluesky handles. This reads each account's recent public posts — who they reply to, quote, mention, or repost — and tallies who comes up most. The middle of the diagram below is the overlap: accounts both handles interact with.

Approximate, on purpose: it looks at each account's most recent public activity (up to 300 posts, 3 pages of 100), not their whole history, and it skips anything the Bluesky moderation service has labelled for hiding. An account that mostly posts originals rather than replying, quoting or mentioning others may turn up with few or no interactions — that's a real result, not a bug. Nothing here is stored; every run asks public.api.bsky.app fresh. Start typing a handle for suggestions from Bluesky's own search. The diagram above is a picture with real avatar photos laid on top of it — long-press or right-click a single avatar for its own "Save Image", or press elsewhere on the diagram (or the "copy graph image" button) for the whole thing at once. That whole-diagram copy is where the photos can't fully follow: it's one flat PNG, and putting a photo inside those bytes needs Bluesky's CDN to let a browser read its pixels back out, which it has not granted in testing so far — an avatar that can't be read that way is a plain initial in the flat copy even though it's a real photo on screen. The copy button also puts a second, richer version on your clipboard alongside the flat image: paste into something that accepts rich text (Docs, Notion, Slack, Mail) and every avatar shows as a real photo there too. Pasting as a plain image (Bluesky itself, most chat apps, Photos) still gets the flat PNG, initials and all.
