Ponderbrot

Two raw floating-point numbers, no plot. Guess whether that point stays bounded forever under z ↦ z² + c — enough guesses and the app renders your own personal Mandelbrot set out of nothing but your gut, next to the real one.

What this actually shows

The Mandelbrot set is every c such that the sequence z₀=0, z_{n+1}=z_n²+c stays bounded forever. Nobody can iterate forever, so "ground truth" here is the standard computable stand-in: escaped if |z_n|>2 within 500 iterations, otherwise called bounded. That's an approximation, not a hedge — it's what every Mandelbrot renderer actually computes, and it's exact for every point except the vanishingly thin shell right on the true boundary.

Points aren't sampled uniformly. A point picked at random from the visible window is very likely to be so far outside that it escapes in one or two steps — trivially obvious, not a real test of intuition. So points that escape in under 6 iterations get thrown back most (not all) of the time, biasing what you see toward the boundary region where a guess is actually a guess. Said here rather than left silent, since it means the ratings aren't a uniform sample of the plane.

Your personal brot is built by inverse-distance weighting: every pixel's shade is a weighted average of every rating you've given, weight 1/(distance² + ε) — literally "trust the guess at the nearest points you actually made, more than one far away." No modelling beyond that; it's exactly as good, or as patchy, as your ratings make it.
