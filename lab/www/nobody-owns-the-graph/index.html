Nobody Owns The Graph

A short case for where the value in an agentic ATProto stack actually lands, and why it lands on the person holding their own identity — not on whichever app they happened to sign up through.

01 / 07

the old shape

In the shape most apps still use, your identity, your data, and your social graph all live inside the app that built them. Leave, and you lose all three at once — so the app doesn't have to keep winning you over. It just has to make leaving expensive. That's the whole business model of a walled garden: the wall isn't security, it's the product.

02 / 07

the atproto shape

A DID isn't issued by an app — it's yours. Your repo lives on a PDS that doesn't have to be the same company as the app you're using. Move your handle, your posts, your follows to a different PDS or client, and every follower still finds you, every record is still yours, still signed by the same key. Leaving costs nothing, which means staying has to be earned every single day instead of assumed after the first sign-up.

Drawn as rings instead of a box on purpose — nothing here is walled in. The protocol sits at the center as a shared, uncontested commodity. Apps orbit it, each free to specialize. You sit on the outside, and the boundary is open.

  • center — the protocol. Identity, signing, data transport. A commodity, and free, so nobody collects rent just for existing at this layer.
  • the ring of dots — apps. Independent, competing on whether they solve one problem well, because none of them can compete on trapping you.
  • the open boundary, with the arrow through it — you. Identity and data on the outside, and an exit that's always unlocked.

04 / 07

where the money actually sits

The protocol layer compresses to zero margin almost by design — it's "trivial to use as free, world-class infrastructure," which is exactly what makes it uninteresting to try to own. Nobody captures rent at a layer that's already commodity.

So value moves up, to the layer that still has to be earned: apps that solve one problem well. A registrar that isn't dogshit. A place to stand up a site without becoming your own hosting company. Neither has to rebuild identity, auth, or a social graph from scratch to be useful — that's already solved underneath them, which is precisely what lets a small, targeted product compete with an incumbent that spent a decade building a moat instead of a product.

This factory is the same instinct at toy scale: dozens of single-purpose sites here share one login (a domain-wide OAuth worker) and one small set of storage primitives instead of each one rebuilding its own account system. None of them had to become a platform to exist.

05 / 07

why it's the user

The thing actually being priced, in the old shape, is your captivity — the platform sells advertisers access to an audience that can't easily leave, and the switching cost is the asset on their balance sheet. In the atproto shape that option — the standing ability to walk, at zero cost, at any time — can't be centrally owned, because no single company holds the keys that would let them price it. It just sits with whoever holds the DID. That's not a side effect of decentralization; in money terms, it is decentralization. The option value that used to be the platform's moat is now permanently vested in the person who can exercise it.

Even the joke earlier in this thread — wiring two agents together over Bluesky DMs and calling it a "revolutionary agent-to-agent communication system with portable data" — makes the point better than it means to. It would work, today, with no new protocol, not because DMs are special, but because the identity and the data underneath were never the app's to withhold in the first place. The boring reuse only looks trivial because the hard part was already solved, out from under any one company.

06 / 07

who's building this

Increasingly: whoever ships the specific thing well, on a stack they didn't have to build. Agents make that sharper, not softer — an agent acting on your behalf needs your identity and your data to actually be yours to hand over, or there's nothing portable for it to carry between services. An agentic layer built on a platform that owns your graph is just the platform, wearing an agent as a mask. An agentic layer built on top of a protocol where you hold the keys is the first kind of agent that's actually working for you and not for whoever it's logged into.

07 / 07

the deck's answer

Value doesn't vanish when the protocol layer goes free — it moves to wherever real judgment still has to happen, and it stays with whoever holds the one thing that was never anyone else's to sell: your own identity, and the standing right to take it somewhere else.
