Ising Sketch

The 2D Ising model is a grid of atoms that can each point only "up" or "down", each one nudged by its four neighbours toward agreeing with them, and by temperature toward disagreeing at random. Feed it a photo as an external field — bright pixels pull spins up, dark pixels pull them down — and Glauber dynamics relax the lattice toward a drawing of it, redrawn one flipped spin at a time.

What's actually happening

Energy is E = −J∑sisj − ∑hisi, summed over neighbouring pairs on a periodic (wrap-around) square lattice, where each pixel's own field hi is set from its brightness — bright pixels pull toward +1, dark pixels toward −1, scaled by the image strength slider. Every sweep visits every site in a random order and applies the Glauber (heat-bath) rule: the local field J∑sj + hi sets the probability of landing spin-up as 1 / (1 + e−2·local/T), so a low temperature makes the lattice follow the image closely and a high one lets thermal noise win, the same competition that gives the real model its phase transition. This square lattice's critical temperature is Tc = 2J / ln(1+√2) ≈ 2.269J — the readout below shows how close the current T is to it, since that is roughly where structure stops holding and dissolves into noise.

Runs entirely in your browser: the image never leaves your device. This is a genuine Metropolis-free Glauber/heat-bath simulation on a periodic square lattice, not a stylised filter pretending to be one — which also means it is not the fastest way to draw a photo in two colours, just the most honest one.
