domino upright

upright dominoes topple along the axis they were stood on, pushed by a falling neighbour that isn't orthogonal to that axis.

generation 0

place mode: tap a cell to cycle empty → four upright orientations → clear. press and drag across cells to lay a chain of dominoes that follows your path, each standing perpendicular to the direction you dragged.

the rule

The Moore neighbourhood has 8 directions; projectivizing it identifies each direction with its opposite, leaving 4 axes — those are the 4 upright orientations. Falling keeps the full 8 directions, since a topple to the northeast is not the same event as one to the southwest.

An upright on axis θ can only ever become one of 2 falling states: falling(θ) or falling(θ+180°) — it can only topple along the axis it is standing on. Each tick, it looks at its 8 neighbours for ones that are falling toward it (a neighbour at relative direction n is falling in direction n+180°). A push whose direction sits within 45° of θ tips it toward falling(θ); within 45° of θ+180° tips it the other way; a push exactly orthogonal to θ (±90°) has no lever arm and is ignored entirely. If it receives valid pushes toward both ends at once — opposite directions cancelling — it stays upright. Falling always becomes fallen (one shared terminal state) after exactly one tick, whichever of the 8 directions it fell in.

place mode stands or clears dominoes: tap one cell to cycle it, or press and drag across several to lay a whole chain — each domino's axis is set from the direction you dragged through it, so a curved path curves the chain with it. push mode lets you shove an upright yourself: press the tile and drag away from its centre — a white arrow previews exactly which of the 8 directions will be used, updating live as you move — then release to commit, since an external push isn't limited to the tile's own axis. The automaton's own propagation rule is the constrained one described above.
