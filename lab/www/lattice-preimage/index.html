lattice preimage

A transformation M ∈ GL(2,ℤ) acting on the integer lattice ℤ² — pull the neighborhood of an image point back through M⁻¹.

Every point of ℤ² is a Gaussian integer x + yi. Choose an integer matrix M with determinant ±1 — that condition (not just "integer entries") is what makes M a bijection of ℤ² onto itself, with an integer inverse M⁻¹. Pick a lattice point p. Its image is q = Mp. The von Neumann neighborhood of q is q itself plus its four orthogonal neighbors (q±(1,0), q±(0,1)) — always an axis-aligned plus, by definition, wherever q lands. This page computes the preimage of that neighborhood: the five points M⁻¹ sends to it. Since M⁻¹ need not preserve the grid's axes, that preimage shape around p is usually a skewed diamond, not a plus.

Growing a tree repeats this construction recursively: starting at the origin, pick a fresh random M ∈ GL(2,ℤ) shared by every point in that recursion level, connect each point in the frontier to the preimage points that level's M induces, then recurse — a new random M for the next level, skipping any point already visited — for up to the given number of levels. Colour marks either distance (in levels) from the origin, or vertex degree — how many edges meet at each point — since a later point's induced neighbor can land back on an already-visited one, so this is a graph that can merge branches together, not strictly a tree. A points-only view is available too.
