Root Yard

Each point on the left is one coefficient ck of a polynomial — drag it anywhere on the complex plane. The right side plots every complex root of the resulting polynomial, recomputed as you move. Degree 1–3 are solved exactly, by the classical linear / quadratic / cubic (Cardano) formulas; degree 4 and up are solved numerically by Durand–Kerner iteration (a quartic formula exists too, but is not built yet — see the numerical fallback's residual to judge how good an approximation you're looking at).

"Permute coefficients" keeps the exact same set of complex values but reassigns which power each one multiplies — same numbers, different polynomial, generally different roots. Numerical roots (degree ≥ 4) show their iteration residual; a residual that stops shrinking usually means two roots have collided (a repeated root), which is the hard case for this method.
