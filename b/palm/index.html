six readings · one repository · no verdict

Your entire posting history — not the last thousand posts, the whole repository — downloaded and read in this tab. Six lines are taken off it and placed against 83 real accounts, giving you a position on a dial that runs from Pan at one end to the Loom at the other. Nothing is uploaded. Nothing is stored.

linereadingpercentile

what this is

Six stylometric measurements over your whole archive, each oriented so that a high number is the more machine-like end.

how the reading is put together

A number on its own is a thin thing to hand someone: two people who both land on 43 have nothing in common except 43. So the reading is a pair, the way a chart is — which line runs furthest toward the machine, and which furthest toward the animal. Six lines give thirty ordered pairs, each with its own name, and with seven bands that is 210 distinct readings rather than seven. The pair is about the shape of your own hand, not the absolute numbers: someone at 12 and someone at 88 can both be Switchboards.

The composite is a percentile of a percentile, and that is deliberate. Averaging six roughly independent percentiles is the central limit theorem applied to your own dial — the mean piles up near 50, and measured across the pool it did exactly that: 80% of accounts fell in two of the seven bands, and Wholly Pan and The Loom were reached by nobody at all. So the mean goes back through the same treatment the axes got, against the pool's distribution of means. Every band is now reachable, and each band's width is honestly its share of the population.

It is not an AI detector, and it cannot be one. Bluesky caps a post at 300 characters, and every published detector degrades badly on text that short — so a per-post verdict here would be invention. What survives at this length is stylometry in aggregate: no single number below means anything, but the error on a mean over tens of thousands of posts falls as √N, which is why the reading is about the account and never about a post. The dial is a percentile among other accounts, not a probability that anything was generated.

where the comparison comes from

The reference pool is built from accounts the seed handle actually replies to, measured exactly the same way — full repository, same code, same fixed budgets. That is a real bias and worth saying plainly: it is a sample of people who post enough to be replied to repeatedly, not a sample of Bluesky.

Chorus is the line that bias bites hardest. The pool was selected by replying to it, so it is made of unusually conversational accounts, and everyone measured against it will look more broadcast-y than they are. Read that spoke as "compared to the people who talk back to you", not as a fact about Bluesky. The other five are only mildly affected; this one is circular by construction.

the reading is public data

com.atproto.sync.getRepo is unauthenticated, so any handle can be read without its owner's involvement. That is why this page reports a position and a confidence and never a verdict — and why the card names a percentile rather than an accusation. Read your own palm. Reading someone else's is between you and them.
