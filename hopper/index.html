You are on a slab in the void. Look up: there is a bucket. In your pocket, a few packs of masons. What this is

A puzzle platformer built on bismuth, the hopper-crystal engine: the same mason agents, the same three rules from real crystal growth, the same thin-film iridescence. Here they are your tools. Every level number is a permalink: same slab, same packs, same bucket, forever.

Packs

Each pack is a specimen's laws — a habit (hopper, plate, tower, stair, twin), a crew, a budget — and a plate it lands as. Deploy it on a brick and the plate is laid on that column's top plane; that plane is the new colony's floor and nothing is ever laid beneath it. Deploying freezes whatever was still growing: what has grown is terrain now. So each deploy is you choosing which plane of the world becomes the platform for the next growth.

The bucket

It hangs where a naive stack of your packs would reach, times a margin, off to one side. You cannot build on it. You have to get above its rim and drop in.

The body

Two layers tall, jumps one. A brick laid where you stand carries you up with it, so standing on a growing plane is a way to rise. Fall into the void and you wake on the slab; the crystal keeps.

Breaking

Any brick can be broken, slab included. Bonds and the crystal's extent maps stay exact, so a growth continues around the hole as if it had never been there.
