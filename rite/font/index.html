A typeface is a set of decisions made once and kept everywhere: how wide an o should be beside an n, where a bar crosses an e, how a stroke thins as it turns. Roll makes every one of those decisions from a seed — and then lets you overrule it.

Each letter here is drawn the way a scribe would: a centreline, swept by a pen. The pen's shape and angle give the contrast; the letter's skeleton gives its character. Spacing follows Tracy's rule of straights and rounds, and every pair is kerned by measuring the white between its shapes. None of it is traced from an existing font.

Drawn, not traced

Every glyph is a Metafont-style skeleton — knots and the directions the pen travels through them — swept by a superellipse nib. The pen's angle and shape give the contrast; unioning and refitting give clean quadratic outlines.

A genome of type

About thirty genes: weight, contrast, stress, x-height, aperture, serifs, terminals, construction. A seed picks an archetype — geometric, grotesque, humanist, old-style, transitional, Didone, slab, rounded — and wanders from it.

Spaced and kerned

Sidebearings follow Tracy's straights-and-rounds method; kerning is measured from each pair's shapes, clustered into classes and written as OpenType GPOS — the way a foundry ships it.
