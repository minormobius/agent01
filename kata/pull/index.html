PULL. A model rope pulled to failure. Each row above is one fibre's place in the rope, drawn along its length: short fibres laid end to end (the gaps are their ends), or one continuous filament. Every stretch of fibre has its own strength, so weak spots exist. Neighbours pass load to each other through friction, up to a limit set by how hard they are pressed together; past it they slip. The rope is stretched step by step, links over their strength break, and the force at the jaws is recorded. Strength is the peak, in units of one continuous strand of the same length.
