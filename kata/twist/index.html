TWIST. A rope's strength is friction, and in our world friction comes from twist: a fibre wound round the core is pressed onto it when pulled. Here are seven elastic fibres, the top end turned, both ends clamped, the yarn stretched, then left alone. Left: the side view, slowly turning. Right: the cross-section half way up. In 3D the turns stay and the core is squeezed. In 4D a fibre's way round the core can slide over it through the spare direction (z here: fibres tint teal and rose as they leave the plane), every whole turn comes off, and the squeeze goes to nothing. Turn jiggle off and the 4D twist stands: it is an equilibrium, an unstable one. A part turn stays in both, because the clamps force it. how it works · the rope that doesn't need twist · kata
