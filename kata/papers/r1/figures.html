Rope after the knot: the ropes, drawn

Every rope lies along w, the direction you cannot see in an ordinary view. Each is drawn three ways. Two cross-sections: the slice x, y, z at two places along the rope, which is what you would see looking at a cut end. One side view: the slice x, y, w, with the rope running up the picture. In a cross-section a thread is a ball, a ribbon is a strip, a tube is a ring and a hose or a hoop is a sphere. Drawn by CAD⁴ from the same trees its tests measure; each heading opens the part there. A plait of three hoses

A hose's cross-section is a ring. Three rings, one inside or beside the next, and they have changed places between the two cuts: one ring has passed through another. From the side, each hose is cut twice and the two halves are ordinary plaits.

A yarn of tubes

Seven rings: one, and six round its wire. Between the cuts the six have turned about the one. This is our twisted yarn with every fibre a tube, and the twist cannot come out.
