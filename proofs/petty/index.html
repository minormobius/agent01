For a polytope, the projection body is a sum of segments, one per facet: ΠK = Σ [−s_F ν_F/2, s_F ν_F/2], with s_F the facet's area and ν_F its normal. A sum of segments has volume Σ |det| over every way of choosing d of them. The product T_r × T_s has r + s + 2 facets, so in ℝ²⁰ that is C(22, 20) = 231 determinants of 20 × 20 integer matrices, all exact. The paper's product formula is not used.

The release's Lean statement product_counterexample claims the quotient equals both 121·C(20,10)/(21·2²⁰) and 22355476/22020096, and that it exceeds 1. The paper's integer certificate is 121·184756 − 21·1048576 = 335380 > 0.

The paper's product test: for r, s ≥ 2,

R(T_r × T_s) / c_{r+s} = (r+1)(s+1)/(r+s+1) · C(r+s, r) · r^r s^s / (r+s)^{r+s}

where c_d = (d+1)·d^d / d! is the simplex value. Tap a cell to see its exact value.

On the ladder, the blue line is the best product of simplices in each dimension, found by dynamic programming over all splits into parts of size at least 2 and rechecked exactly up to n = 30. It stays at or below the simplex until n = 20, where 10 + 10 wins. After that it pulls away exponentially. The paper's corollary says some λ > 1 works. This page's own computation puts the best rate at e^{max_a (log c_a)/a − 1} ≈ 1.034 per dimension, reached with factors of size 12 or 13. The orange line is Petty's ellipsoid minimum and the grey line is the cube, 2ⁿ.

Shadows and the projection body

Shine a light on a convex body K from direction u and measure the area of its shadow. Do that for every u and you get a function. It is the support function of a second convex body, the projection body ΠK (Minkowski, 1903). The ratio R(K) = |ΠK| / |K|^{n−1} does not change under any affine map, so it measures shape alone. Two old questions ask for its extremes.

Petty (1971): the minimum. Ellipsoids should give the smallest R, with value κ_{n−1}ⁿ κ_n^{2−n}, where κ_j is the volume of the unit ball in ℝʲ. Chen, Feng, Li, Xi and Xu proved this in ℝ³ in 2026. Brannen (1996): the maximum. Simplices should give the largest R, with value c_n = (n+1)nⁿ/n!. Tetrahedra do maximise in ℝ³ (same paper). In the plane, R(K) = |K − K| / |K| lies between 4 and 6 for every K. The minimum 4 is reached by every centrally symmetric shape, not only ellipses, which is why Petty's conjecture starts at n = 3.

The 2026 claims

Two papers dated 24 September 2026, released on 6 October in a collection of 722 model-written papers, make the claims below.

  • Petty in every dimension n ≥ 4, with ellipsoids the only minimisers. Combined with the ℝ³ theorem of Chen et al., the conjecture holds for every n ≥ 3.
  • Brannen fails: T₁₀ × T₁₀ beats the simplex in ℝ²⁰, by the factor 22355476/22020096 ≈ 1.0152. Products of simplices also win by an exponential factor in every large dimension.

The second paper is candid about priority. Feng, Hu, Liu and Xu had already given counterexamples in every dimension d ≥ 9, using polytopes with at most d + 2 facets. What is new is a direct product construction whose value is exact.

What Lean covers

Both papers are in the release's formalization catalogue. Its scope note says the formalization proves Petty's inequality with the equality case for every n ≥ 4, and the dimension-20 counterexample. It does not cover the Lutwak–Petty family or the exponential excess. The counterexample's statement, verbatim:

theorem product_counterexample :
    (IsCompact productWitness ∧ Convex ℝ productWitness ∧
      (interior productWitness).Nonempty) ∧
    normalizedProjectionVolume productWitness / simplexConstant 20 =
      (121 * (Nat.choose 20 10 : ℝ)) / (21 * (2 : ℝ) ^ 20) ∧
    normalizedProjectionVolume productWitness / simplexConstant 20 =
      (22355476 : ℝ) / 22020096 ∧
    1 < normalizedProjectionVolume productWitness / simplexConstant 20 ∧
    (volume (projectionBody productWitness)).toReal >
      simplexConstant 20 * (volume productWitness).toReal ^ 19

Here simplexConstant d = (d+1)·d^d/d!, and projectionBody K is the set of y with ⟨u, y⟩ ≤ ‖u‖·(shadow volume) for every u. And Petty:

theorem petty_projection_volume (n : ℕ) (hn : 4 ≤ n)
    (K : Set (Space n)) (hK : IsConvexBody K) :
    pettyConstant n ≤ projectionRatio K ∧
      (projectionRatio K = pettyConstant n ↔ IsEllipsoid K)

with pettyConstant n = κ(n−1)^n · κ(n)^(2−n). We did not build or run the Lean.

What this page checks

petty.js is the only copy of the maths, and petty.selftest.mjs holds it to 2,926 checks.

  • The counterexample by brute force. The 22 facet vectors of T₁₀ × T₁₀ go into the zonotope volume formula: 231 exact 20 × 20 determinants. The quotient comes out exactly 22355476/22020096, with no use of the product formula. The same brute force matches the formula on ten smaller products and three more 20-dimensional splits.
  • The simplex value. |ΠT_d| = (d+1)/((d−1)!)^d, from the facet zonotope for d ≤ 12. The certificate integer is 335380.
  • Every dimension. No product of simplices beats the simplex below ℝ²⁰. This is exact for every two-factor split, and for the best split of each n ≤ 30.
  • The facet lemma itself. In the plane and in space, the formula ½ Σ|⟨s_F ν_F, u⟩| matches the measured shadow. Random polygons give 4 ≤ R₂ ≤ 6.
  • Reference values. In space: tetrahedron 18, prism 12, octahedron 9, cube 8, and hulls of many sphere points approaching 3π²/4 ≈ 7.402.

Not checked: Petty's inequality in ℝ⁴ and up. It is a statement about every convex body, and its proof is analysis (spherical harmonics, a strict norm estimate, a fixed-point argument). Shapes can only illustrate it.
