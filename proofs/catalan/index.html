Two independent routes to the same digits. One is Ramanujan's formula G = (π/8)·log(2 + √3) + (3/8)·Σ (n!)²/((2n)!(2n+1)²). The other is the defining alternating series, which converges painfully slowly until it is sped up with the Cohen–Rodriguez Villegas–Zagier acceleration. Matching digits are no proof of anything. Irrationality is a statement about every possible fraction, which is why it took until 2026.

How the proof works. It builds determinants Δ_N of size 48N whose entries mix 1, G and ζ(2), with ζ(2) cancelling exactly. If G were a fraction, every Δ_N would be rational, and counting the primes in its denominator forces log|Δ_N|, suitably scaled, to stay above −2.29084. An unconditional analytic estimate pins it below −2.290939875. Both cannot hold, and the room between them is 0.0000999. Both bounds rest on finite certificates printed in the paper, and the next two tabs replay them.

The analytic bound comes down to the highest point of two explicit one-variable functions, X and Y, for each case κ. The paper finds every stationary point exactly. It builds the derivatives' numerator polynomials over ℚ, counts their roots on each interval by Descartes' rule, traps each root in a bracket of width 2·10⁻¹⁰, and bounds the function at all fifty candidates. Press replay to redo all of it in exact arithmetic, with 60-digit logarithms.

The arithmetic bound needs some Δ_N to be nonzero. The paper reduces the growing 48p × 48p determinant, for large primes p, to three fixed rational 48 × 48 matrices: ℬ₀, ℬ₀ + ℬ₁ and ℬ₀ − ℬ₁. It builds every entry by rational recurrences and certifies invertibility by Gaussian elimination mod 101, printing all 144 pivots. Replay rebuilds ℬ from the recipe, redoes the elimination, and computes the three determinants exactly over ℚ as well.

The constant

Catalan's constant G = 1 − 1/9 + 1/25 − 1/49 + … ≈ 0.9159655942 turns up across mathematics: in counting domino tilings, in the volume of the hyperbolic figure-eight knot complement, and in a long list of integrals. Its sibling ζ(2) = 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + … equals π²/6 and has been known to be irrational since 1794. Whether G is a fraction stayed open. The best results showed only that at least one of a small family of similar numbers must be irrational, without saying which (Rivoal and Zudilin, 2003; down to five values by Lai and Zhou, 2022).

The 2026 claim

A paper dated 24 September 2026, written by an internal OpenAI model and released on 6 October in a collection of 722, proves that G is irrational. The determinants it builds behave like squeezed rationals. If G were p/q they could neither be too small (denominators) nor too large (analysis), and the two estimates miss each other by about 0.0001. The paper also deduces irrationality of some hyperbolic volumes. It notes that Sun has announced an independent proof of the same statement.

How it was checked

The release's Lean library contains a development for this theorem: 981 files and about 574,000 lines, with no sorry. It also contains a Comparator challenge whose statement is the definition itself:

theorem catalan_irrational :
    Irrational (∑' j : ℕ, (-1 : ℝ) ^ j
      / ((2 * j + 1 : ℕ) : ℝ) ^ 2)

But the release's formalization catalogue does not list this paper, so the index on this site counts it as not formalized. We did not build or run that Lean. The paper is a preprint.

What this page checks

catalan.js is the only copy of the maths, and catalan.selftest.mjs holds it to 166 checks. It replays both of the paper's finite certificates exactly.

From the nonvanishing certificate:

  • All 144 pivots modulo 101 and both row swaps match the printed table.
  • The three determinants are nonzero over ℚ, computed exactly (numerators of about 2,000 digits).
  • Each determinant reduced mod 101 equals ± the product of the paper's pivots.

From the barrier certificate:

  • The four numerator polynomials have the paper's degrees (36, 24, 13, 9).
  • Every Descartes count matches, and every one of the 43 root brackets has a sign change.
  • All 50 tabulated values and both norm terms hold, each within 10⁻¹² of its printed bound.
  • The final sum comes out exactly −2.290939875.

Not checked here: the arithmetic lower bound −2.29084. It needs the prime number theorem and the paper's denominator analysis. Also not checked: the step from the fixed matrices to the growing determinants. Those are the proof's words, not its numbers.
