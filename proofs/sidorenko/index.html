H is the incidence graph of this complex: 13 point vertices and 22 face vertices, with an edge whenever a point lies on a face. That makes 35 vertices and 66 edges, bipartite. Every pair of points that share a face lies on exactly two faces. The paper turns that double occurrence into a negative sign correlation.

t(H, G) counts every map of H's 35 vertices into G that sends edges to edges, repeats allowed, divided by n³⁵. Summing out each face first leaves a sum over the 13 points with one factor per face, the number of common neighbours of its three points. The points form a triangulated sphere, so the sum splits into small pieces (treewidth 5). Graphs up to 10 vertices are compared exactly in integers, and larger ones in floating point.

Every small graph here obeys Sidorenko, usually by a wide margin, because maps that repeat vertices inflate small counts. Complete graphs creep down toward 1 and stay above it. The paper's host is not small. It is sampled from a kernel built on symmetric matrices over a finite field 𝔽_q, in a large fixed dimension, and only works once q is large enough. Nobody has written it down.

In the limit, graphs become kernels: W(x, y) ≥ 0 on two probability spaces. Then t(H, W) is the expected product of W over H's 66 incidences, with independent samples at the points and at the faces, and Sidorenko asks for t(H, W) ≥ (E W)⁶⁶. "Descend" runs a random local search that tries to push the ratio below 1. It always slides to the constant kernel, where the ratio is exactly 1. Lovász proved that no counterexample lives near the constant (2011), and the paper's kernel is far away, over matrices in 𝔽_q.

The conjecture

For finite graphs, t(H, G) is the probability that a random map from H's vertices to G's sends every edge to an edge. In a random graph of edge density p it is p^{|E(H)|}. Sidorenko conjectured in 1991–93 that bipartite H can never do worse: t(H, G) ≥ p(G)^{|E(H)|} for every G. Trees, even cycles, complete bipartite graphs and hypercubes were proved, and so were many families built by gluing and reflection. The conjecture became one of the central open problems of extremal graph theory.

The 2026 claim

A paper dated 23 September 2026, released on 6 October in a collection of 722 model-written papers, disproves it. H is the incidence graph of 22 triples on 13 points. The proof builds a finite kernel from symmetric matrices over 𝔽_q whose differences have rank exactly half the dimension, with signed determinants. Each of the 33 point pairs lies in two faces, and that gives a negative correction. A long estimate then controls the singular configurations. A sampled finite graph inherits the deficit. The paper also disproves the forcing conjecture of Skokan and Thoma for the same H.

What Lean covers

The release's Lean library proves the counterexample. The forcing consequence is outside its scope. The paper is not in the formalization catalogue. The statement, verbatim:

def faces : Fin 22 → Finset (Fin 13) := ![
  {0,1,3}, {0,1,9}, {0,2,3}, {0,2,9}, {1,3,10}, {1,7,10},
  {1,7,12}, {1,9,12}, {2,3,4}, {2,4,9}, {3,4,11}, {3,10,11},
  {4,8,9}, {4,8,11}, {5,6,7}, {5,6,11}, {5,7,10}, {5,10,11},
  {6,7,12}, {6,8,11}, {6,8,12}, {8,9,12}]

theorem main : ∃ size : ℕ, 0 < size ∧ ∃ host : SimpleGraph (Fin size),
    (∃ left right, host.Adj left right) ∧
    homDensity H host < (edgeDensity host) ^ 66

Here homDensity counts all edge-preserving maps divided by size³⁵, and edgeDensity is 2|E|/size². The page's counts use exactly these definitions. It is an existence statement: the host is not written down. We did not build or run the Lean.

What this page checks

sidorenko.js is the only copy of the maths, and sidorenko.selftest.mjs holds it to 205 checks.

  • The paper's Proposition on the complex, all four items.
    • All 33 pairs lie in exactly two faces, and every opposite-face entry of Table 1 is right.
    • The face-neighbour graph is connected.
    • Each of the six bit classes has 11 faces covering all 13 points, and the exposure orders in Table 2 work as printed.
    • Every a_e ≥ 1/3, and a_{0,2} = 1.
    • The degree table is right, and only {4,10}, {6,7} and {8,12} share signatures.
    • Colour refinement separates every vertex.
  • The sign identity E_η(1 + cηa)(1 + cηb) = 1 + ab for every choice of signs.
  • Counting. Elimination equals a direct 13-point sum on every graph on 3 vertices. Hom(H, K₂) = 2 by brute force, and Hom(H, P₃) = 2¹³ + 2²². Two elimination orders agree.
  • Small hosts obey Sidorenko, exactly. All preset hosts up to 10 vertices pass, and so do 30 random graphs. K₃ to K₁₁ decrease toward 1. Random 2 × 2 and 3 × 3 kernels never go below 1.

An observation of this page, not of the paper: the complex is a triangulated 2-sphere. It has χ = 13 − 33 + 22 = 2, every link is a cycle, and it is orientable. That is why it can be drawn flat, and why the counting splits so well.

Not checked: the existence of the host. That is the paper's analysis: rank layers over 𝔽_q, Lagrangian intersection counts and a singular-tail bound, with q → ∞. The Lean proof covers it. Nothing on this page could find the host by search, and the hosts tab shows why.
