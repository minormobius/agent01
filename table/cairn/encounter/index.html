Encounter oracle

Cairn publishes no challenge rating, no XP budget and no encounter table — difficulty is the Warden's call, made in the fiction. So this doesn't look difficulty up. It plays the fight, thousands of times, against the actual sheets your seed rolled, using Cairn's own combat rules, and reports what happened. Then it searches the bestiary for the encounters that land where you want them.

What this number is, and is not

It is a floor, not a forecast. The model fights to the last body: no terrain, no tricks, no talking, no torches thrown, nobody running. Cairn is a game about avoiding exactly that fight, so a real table will almost always do better than the number here. Treat it as the cost of a scene going badly with nobody getting clever.

  • Modelled: the DEX save to act in round one, auto-hitting attacks, armour subtracted from every hit, overflow damage into STR, the STR save against critical damage, death at 0 STR, paired dice keeping the highest, blast hitting everyone, and enemy morale saves at the first casualty and at half losses.
  • Magic and monster abilities are modelled — . A basilisk wards off attacks and petrifies, a banshee's wail takes out anyone who fails a WIL save, a troll gets back up, an owlbear tears armour off, and a party carrying a Sleep spellbook can read it — paying a slot of Fatigue to do so, which in a full pack costs them their HP. Each verdict lists what it simulated and what it could not.
  • Still not modelled: retreat, hirelings, detachments, terrain, healing between fights, the Scars table firing mid-fight, and every ability whose effect is a conversation or a door. Nine of Cairn's hundred spells have combat mechanics; the other ninety-one are why people play it, and none of them appear in these numbers.
  • The bands are ours, not Cairn's. Cairn publishes no difficulty scale. Inventing one that looked official would be a lie, so: routine is under a 12% chance of anyone going down, risky above it, deadly means a real chance of losing someone, lethal means the party is wiped at least a quarter of the time.
  • "Delves survived" is our stand-in for levels. Cairn has none, and it puts magic entirely in objects — so a character's power is what they are carrying, and carrying is capped at ten slots. One delve advances both halves at once: loot found, and a coin-flip chance of a scar, which raises a maximum. Advancement saturates by about the third delve, after which a veteran is not accumulating but choosing what to leave behind.
  • A full pack is modelled, and it is brutal. "Anyone carrying a full inventory is reduced to 0 HP" — so encumbrance is not flavour here, and there is a point where picking up one more shield makes a party measurably worse. See what a slot is worth.
