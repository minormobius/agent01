## Why this system and not another

The ask was a **map** and **classes with skill trees**. Of the openly licensed
systems surveyed on the parent landing page:

- **Pathfinder 2e** is the best mechanical fit — feats form a real prerequisite
  DAG and the grid is load-bearing — and it is unusable here. Its rules are
  genuinely ORC-licensed, but Paizo publishes **no SRD**, so every convenient
  corpus is either Foundry's bespoke agreement with Paizo or the community
  repos running on Paizo's **Community Use Policy**, which is non-commercial
  and revocable. That is the same objection that put Daggerheart at "no".
- **Mausritter** has the best map in the survey and **no classes at all**.
- **SRD 5.2.1** is the only one with both layers *and* a clean, irrevocable,
  downloadable corpus.

It also brings something Cairn could not: an **official** encounter budget.
Cairn publishes no challenge rating, which is why `table/cairn/`'s oracle had to
invent Toll and Swing and label them as ours. Here there is a published answer,
so for the first time the question can be *graded* rather than replaced.

## Three licence rules that are different from Cairn's

1. **CC BY, not CC BY-SA.** No share-alike obligation on our code. Do not copy
   Cairn's footer here, or this one there.
2. **The attribution statement is fixed and exclusive.** It must appear
   verbatim, and the SRD's legal page forbids *any other* credit to Wizards.
   One statement, nowhere else, nothing added.
3. **Trademarks are not licensed** — CC BY 4.0 §2(b)(2). Hence `srd5`, hence no
   logo and no game name in the branding. The one claim the SRD expressly
   permits is "compatible with fifth edition" or "5E compatible".

## Parsing a PDF is not scraping a site

`table/cairn/tools/` reads HTML with real table markup: a broken parse throws.
This reads **typeset** text — two justified columns, hyphens inserted at line
breaks, a running head on every page — and **its failures come back looking like
fluent English**. A creature quietly loses its CR; an attack is truncated one
word before its damage; a swarm is welded onto the creature above it. Every one
of those still renders, still reads correctly, and still simulates.

So the selftest is not decoration, and it is built on three moves:

- **Reconciliation** — count the same thing two ways. 330 stat blocks against
  330 `AC` lines is what caught the swarms; 423 entries saying `Attack Roll:`
  against 423 parsed attacks is what caught the truncation.
- **External grading** — check against knowledge that is *not* in the parse.
  The CR-to-XP table is written out by hand in the test precisely because CR and
  XP are captured off one line by one regex; grading their relationship tests
  both captures with outside knowledge.
- **Artefact sweeps** — search the whole corpus for the specific damage
  typesetting does, rather than spot-checking a creature and calling it good.

### What those checks actually found

Each of these looked fine on the page:

| Found | Cost |
|---|---|
| type line reads `Large Swarm of Tiny Beasts` — lowercase words inside the type | all **7 swarms** merged into the creature above |
| `Piercing damage.` wraps to the start of a line and looks exactly like a bolded entry name | **80 of 423 attacks** truncated before their damage, with a phantom action named "Piercing damage" holding the remains |
| the CR line is printed **two ways** — `CR 3 (XP 700)` and `CR 3 (700 XP)` | **4 dragons** lost CR and XP |
| hyphenation across a **page** break, which a per-page pass cannot rejoin | `5-foot- wide` left in the text |
| the justifier splits digits — `11,70 0`, `7 ,200` | the level-17 XP budget read as 11,700 → 70 |
| one ability save printed with no sign (`Int 6 -2 2`) | one creature's whole ability block |
| dice printed with an **en dash** (`1d4–1`) | the Jackal's bite |

### One error in the document itself

SRD 5.2.1 prints the Archmage as `CR 12 (XP 8,000)`. CR 12 is worth **8,400**,
and the Erinyes — also CR 12 — prints 8,400 correctly. This is an erratum in the
source, and **the data keeps 8,000**: a corpus that silently disagrees with the
book someone is reading at the table is a worse failure than being wrong in the
same way the source is. The selftest pins it as the *only* known discrepancy, so
a later revision fixing it (or adding another) shows up as a test failure rather
than a stale exception.

## Things that will bite you here

- **Do not "fix" the source.** Errata are recorded and surfaced, never patched
  into the data. See above.
- **`clean()` is per page, `mend()` is document-wide, and the split matters.**
  Page furniture must go before the pages are joined; hyphenation must be
  repaired after, or a word broken across a page boundary can never be rejoined.
- **A threshold is not a test.** `attacks.length > 250` passed happily while 80
  attacks were missing, because 343 is also more than 250. Reconcile against a
  count derived a different way.
- **Not every attack has dice, and not every attack has damage.** The Awakened
  Shrub deals a flat `1 Slashing`; the Roper's tentacle deals none at all and
  only grapples. Both are real, both are kept — the Roper's bonus and its
  60-foot reach still matter to a map.
- **`monsters.js` is 620 KB.** It gzips well, but if a page only needs stat
  lines, do not make it parse every action's prose.

## Still to build

- **The map.** The grid: squares, movement, reach against range, opportunity
  attacks, cover, difficult terrain, and the cone/emanation/sphere templates.
  Every distance is already parsed and checked to be a whole number of five-foot
  squares, which is the precondition.
- **The audit.** Whether the official XP budget predicts what actually happens.
- **Classes and the tree.** And an honest page about it: the SRD's tree is
  **thin** — twelve classes, one subclass each, seventeen feats, and the only
  genuine prerequisite chain is the Warlock's invocations. Say so; do not dress
  a level table up as a skill tree.
