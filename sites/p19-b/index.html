Spur & Siding

A patch of country, a handful of towns, and a budget that does not renew. Draw the track, throw the switches, keep the trains earning more than the rails cost you.

Budget: $0 Track laid: 0 Trains running: 0 Revenue earned: $0

How to lay track: tap a town to select it, then tap a second town to build the straight line between them — the cost depends on distance and what the line crosses. Tap the first town again to cancel. Water needs a bridge, high ground needs grading, and both cost more per mile than open plains.

Switches: a town with three or more lines gets a small diamond marker beside it — that is the points lever. Tap the diamond to throw it. A through train takes whichever line the points are set for; get it wrong and your train heads somewhere you didn't intend, same as the real thing. A dead-end town just backs the train up.

Trains earn a fare for every stretch of line they finish running, but the network itself has upkeep — track costs a small amount every few seconds whether a train is on it or not. If revenue can't keep up with upkeep, the budget bleeds out.

  • plains — cheap to lay
  • forest — a bit dearer
  • high ground — needs grading
  • water — needs a bridge, priciest of all

Costs and fares are this page's own estimate, not a real engineering model — treat the numbers as relative, not literal.
