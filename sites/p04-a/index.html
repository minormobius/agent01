How much life?

A stack chart of every kind of life in the universe, Big Bang to heat death. Each layer is one form of life; a layer's height is a rough estimate of quality of experience × quantity — its "amount of life" — plotted on a log scale, because linear would make bacteria alone a thousand pixels tall and everything else invisible.

This is an estimate built from very rough numbers, not a measurement — nobody can count subjective experience. Treat the shapes as "roughly this order of magnitude, in roughly this proportion," not as data. See "how these numbers were guessed" below for the actual assumptions, and disagree with them freely.

Humans~8×10⁹ people~6Current population. Quality is the least defensible number on this whole page — it tries to average unimaginable variance (joy, suffering, everything between) into one figure, which is a real distortion, not just a rounding error.
Speculative future lifeunknownunknownModeled as one optimistic scenario — descendants spreading through the galaxy while stars still burn, then a long fade through the degenerate and black-hole eras. Toggle it off to see the alternative: we could just as easily be the whole story.

The chart plots log10(quantity × quality) per layer, stacked. That means total stack height tracks orders of magnitude and is dominated by whichever layer is largest — it is not a literal sum of experience, and "can quality be compared this way across species" is an open, contested question that this page just picks an answer to. The point of the chart is the shape of the guess, not a number to cite.
