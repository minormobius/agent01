Domino Field

A grid of dominoes, each standing on one of four axes. Push one over and it may knock its neighbors down in turn — a cellular automaton, not an animation: every topple is computed from the actual state of the eight cells around it, each tick.

The rule, precisely

Every cell is empty, upright on one of 4 axes, falling in one of 8 directions, or fallen (one terminal state — direction is kept only so it renders lying the right way). The 4 axes are the four lines through the 8-direction Moore neighborhood: E–W, NE–SW, N–S, NW–SE.

Falling always becomes fallen on the next tick, no exceptions.

Upright becomes falling in one of its two directions perpendicular to its own axis — a domino topples sideways off its long edge, never off the end. It falls direction F if some neighbor is falling straight at it in a direction that is neither orthogonal (±90°) nor opposite (180°) to F. If a push qualifies for both of its two directions at once — which happens for any diagonal push, since a diagonal is at 45°/135° to both — that counts as being pushed in opposite directions at once, and it stays upright. Net result: a straight-on hit topples it cleanly; a diagonal, orthogonal, or opposing hit does not. That's one honest reading of a genuinely terse rule, spelled out rather than left to guesswork — see BRIEF.md for the alternative readings that were considered and rejected.

Runs entirely in your browser on a plain 2D canvas: nothing here is sent anywhere, and nothing about the physics is faked — every frame redraws from the grid's actual current state.
