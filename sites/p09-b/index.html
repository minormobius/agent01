Agentic ATProto

Agents are about to act on our behalf, constantly. This is a rough deck about one question: when they do, where does the value get captured — and why does an ATProto-shaped agent put it in your hands instead of a platform's?

scroll ↓

The old shape

"An AI agent for X" has always meant the same trade: it asks a platform for access to your account, on the platform's terms, through a door the platform can close.

  • Identity is issued by the platform, and dies with the account
  • Your data lives in their database, readable however they allow
  • Access is an API key or a scraped session — theirs to price, throttle, or revoke
  • The agent answers to the platform's rules first, and you second

Every agent built this way is a guest in someone else's house. The house can change the terms at any time, and often does.

What ATProto changes

Two things stop belonging to the platform: your identity (a DID) and your data (a repo, on a PDS you can move without asking). An agent that wants to act for you asks you for one narrow OAuth scope — not the platform for a master key.

The seam just moved. It used to sit between you and the platform; now it sits between you and the agent, and you own both sides of it.

Composability is the actual pitch

Because identity and data are already portable and public by default, small focused tools plug into the same rails without an integration meeting: a domain registrar, a static-hosting tool, a poll, a small factory that builds tenant sites like this one — same DID, same repo, no API key traded between two companies.

marque.at and wisp.place are two live examples: neither had to build the other's account system to talk to it. That is the whole trick, and it is why "smaller, targeted, does-one-thing-well" is a viable shape again instead of a disadvantage against a platform with a bigger moat.

Why the value lands on users

Once identity and data can't be owned by the tool, the tool can't hold you hostage to keep you around — it has to keep being worth using on its own merits. The old moat was "we have your graph and your posts." That moat is gone by construction, not by policy.

  • Switching cost drops — a new agent reads the same repo, day one
  • Competition moves to who solves the problem best, not who has the most lock-in
  • Small, targeted tools can thrive without needing to become platforms themselves

This lab is a small proof of the same idea: most of its sites need no login at all, and the few that save anything write into the visitor's own repo — never into a database this factory owns.

What this deck isn't

No TAM slide, no funding ask, no logo soup — because "where's the value captured" isn't a valuation question here, it's an architecture question, and the architecture already answers it: value sits wherever the seam is, and ATProto puts the seam at your own repo, not a platform's database.
