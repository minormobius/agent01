Hyperbolic Shoal

A playable riff on Escher's Circle Limit III. Every row of fish swims nose‑to‑tail along its own straight line through the centre — a genuine geodesic of the Poincaré disk. Pick a row, drag it along its track, and watch it shrink smoothly toward the rim: that shrinking is not a style choice, it's the disk model telling the truth about a hyperbolic plane packed into a finite circle.

Tap a row below, or drag directly on its fish in the disk. Release and the row snaps back into exact nose‑to‑tail alignment — the tiling always resets to a valid one.

What's real here, what's stylised

The geometry is real: positions come from an actual Möbius translation of the Poincaré disk, and fish size is scaled by the disk's own conformal factor, so a row genuinely tiles head‑to‑tail at every step, not just at the one frame you see. What's stylised: Escher's rows are gently curved arcs meeting the rim at a slant, tuned so three colours meet nose‑to‑nose‑to‑nose; these rows are straight diameters, the simplest true hyperbolic geodesic, and neighbouring rows only truly interact at the centre point they share. Rows near your dragged one wobble a little as you pass through the middle — a visual nod to "everything must move together," not a full rigid‑tiling solve.
