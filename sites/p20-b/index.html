Hyperbolic Fish

A hyperbolic tiling of fish in a Poincaré disk, after M. C. Escher's Circle Limit III. Grab any fish and drag — the whole tiling swims with it, because a rigid tiling has no other way to move.

Tap a fish to pick its row.
row selected

Drag anywhere to pan the whole tiling. Tap one fish to select the geodesic row running through it (its nose-to-tail line), then use the arrows — or drag along that line — to swim just that direction; the rest of the pattern is dragged along with it, tile for tile, to stay tiled.

Approximate, on purpose: this tiles regular octagons (three meeting at every corner, the {8,3} tessellation Circle Limit III's fish are built on) with straight hyperbolic "rows." Escher's actual fish swim along gently curved lines for visual flow — a real difference, not a bug. The pre-built patch of tiling is finite, so dragging far enough eventually reaches its edge.
