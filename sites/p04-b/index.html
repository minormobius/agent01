the amount of life

Every form of life the universe has held or might ever hold, stacked from the Big Bang to heat death — one band per kind, sized by how many of them there were times how rich their experience probably was. Scrub the chart.

Read this before the numbers below. Every value here is an order-of-magnitude guess, not a measurement — population figures for extinct species and anything past roughly 2100 CE are estimates or outright speculation, clearly marked where they get speculative. "Quality of experience" is a number I assigned on a scale I made up; a different reasonable person would place it differently. Treat this as one way of thinking about the shape of the question, not a result.

The horizontal axis is not drawn to a single scale — see "how this is calculated" below for why, and what that trades away.

amount of life = quantity (how many, at once) × quality (how rich the experience, 0–20 scale)  →  stacked as log₁₀(amount)

Move over the chart, or drag on a phone, to read a moment in time.

the axis is bent, deliberately

A true straight scale, seconds since the Big Bang all the way to heat death, would put every band on this chart — bacteria through humans — inside about 0.1% of the width. On that scale the whole of biology is a single pixel and the rest of the chart is future speculation. So the axis is drawn in three log-scaled pieces, each given a fixed share of the width regardless of its real duration: Big Bang → first life (~18%), first life → now (~42%, where every real band lives), and now → heat death (~40%). The tick labels say what's really being compressed at each point — the honest reading is still "life is a rounding error against cosmic time," it's just a rounding error you can actually see the shape of.

why the stack is log-scaled too

Bacteria alone (~5×10³⁰ cells) outnumber humans (~8×10⁹) by twenty orders of magnitude. Stack the raw numbers and every band except bacteria draws as a flat line at zero. So each band's height is log₁₀(quantity × quality), not the product itself — which keeps every band visible, but means the chart is not a literal sum you could read a total off.
