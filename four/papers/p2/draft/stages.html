Supplement S1: how the model was built, and what each stage changed

The model in this paper was not written down once and run. It was built in stages, from 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-27. Each stage replaced a guess, a convention or a cruder method with something computed, then ran the cascade again. The main text gives only the final method. This supplement records the route, for two reasons.

First, when a result survives each step up in sophistication, that is evidence in its own right: a convergence test at the level of the whole model rather than one grid. When a result does not survive, the reader should know it was an artefact of a cruder step. Some did not survive. A cycle on ceryllium-8 was found, withdrawn, reopened and closed for good within two days, and only the last of those rests on something that cannot move: a variational bound.

Second, a sequence of improvements can look like tuning until the answer looks right. Against that, each stage below states whether a decision rule was fixed before its results came in. 14 of the 17 stages had none: their results were read as they came. That is said plainly rather than reconstructed after the fact.

What enters this record. A stage enters when it changed a stated result, when it was a check that could have failed, or when it introduced or replaced a convention. Bugs found and fixed on the way are not stages; they are in the code’s history. Each stage lists what it replaced and by what, the runs behind it, what moved (with the earlier value and where it was recorded), and what held. The earlier values are historical: by their nature they are no longer in the data. Of the 50 entries, 23 are marked ✓: the value given is recomputed from the committed data each time the paper is built, and the build stops if they disagree. 0 marked ⧗ waits on a run in progress.
