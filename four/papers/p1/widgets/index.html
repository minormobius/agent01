The charge law, by hand

Companions to paper 1, Atoms in four spatial dimensions with a 1/r interaction: a self-consistent periodic table. With the force between charges pinned at 1/r in four dimensions there's no shell theorem: a cloud of charge round an atom doesn't act as if it sat at the centre. First, why that law: try the others. Then what it does. Every toy runs on the paper's own code and baked table.

1 · Choose a law

One electron, one nucleus, four dimensions. Under a pull −1/rp its levels group into shells only when p = 1: 2s sits exactly with 2p, and 3s with 3p and 3d. That grouping is what makes shells of 2, 8, 18, 32 and the table's rows. Move p off 1 and the shells come apart.

p

each shell's levels against its own s level, in percent of its binding: s, p, d; at p = 1 they all sit on the dashed line

Gauss's law would give −α/r² instead, and that has no length in it at all. Below α = ½ nothing binds. Above it the electron falls to the centre: its lowest level is set by how close to the nucleus the calculation is allowed to look, and moves without limit as that point moves in.

the lowest level |ε| (hartree) against the grid's inner end r₀: a settled atom would be a flat line; 1/r₀² dashed

3 · A neutral atom's far pull

In our world a neutral atom is invisible from outside: its nucleus and its cloud cancel exactly, by the shell theorem. In four dimensions the cloud's pull falls short of the nucleus's push, by a little that dies away as the cube of the distance. Walk an electron away from the atom: what it feels approaches −S/8s³, with S = Σ⟨r²⟩ over the atom's electrons.
