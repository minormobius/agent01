Bluminium

Bl₂Px₃: element 15 and pxygen, the corundum of four dimensions. Two candidate crystals, still unranked, and a playground for doping them with a d ion to see whether this world has a ruby, or a tunable laser.

The crystal

The ionic model's search found two cells within a fraction of an electronvolt of each other, z2-0 and z2-1. Plane-wave density-functional theory hasn't separated them yet. Each tile shows a cell's shadow, coloured by w, or the slice a 3D creature would see at a chosen w. The dopant playground

Ruby is corundum with one aluminium in a hundred swapped for chromium. Chromium's three d electrons sit in the field of the six oxygens around them, and their levels decide what light the crystal holds. Here any 3dⁿ ion can go on any Bluminium site. The page solves its levels exactly, from every arrangement of n electrons in the d shell's 9 orbitals with both spins, each time a control moves.

What s and β are, and how far to trust them

Each pxygen is a point charge −2. In 4D each one's electron cloud also leaks a field of its own, because the shell theorem fails, and the leak pulls the d electrons back toward it (untick it to see the bare charges). Point charges are known to come out low. On ruby the same model gives 0.27 of the measured splitting, so the field gets a factor s. A free ion's repulsion also shrinks in a crystal, so the repulsion gets a factor β. Ruby fixes both: s = , β = put its broad ⁴T₂ band at 2.23 eV and its sharp ²E line at 1.786 eV. Carrying them to a 4D crystal is an assumption, not a result. Inside ours, titanium on the same site at ruby's s overshoots its measured band by about ×1.4, so expect at least that much play. The real test would be a Hartree–Fock calculation of the dopant and its neighbours, which isn't built.

What to look for. The 4D chromium analogue (3d³, element 28) has no visible ruby: its terms are squeezed, and the first spin flip sits 0.17 eV above the ground term, against 2.2 eV in ours, so its sharp line is in the mid-infrared. The titanium analogue (3d¹, element 26) on z2-0 splits into two sets of levels with a 0.7 eV gap between them, the shape of a tunable infrared laser; on z2-1's sites the gap closes up. And 3d² (element 27) breaks Hund's first rule as a free ion: its ground state is a spin singlet, not a triplet. That comes from the 4D d shell itself, not the charge law, since Gauss's 1/r² gives the same order. Turn s down to 0 to see it.
