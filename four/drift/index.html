Drift golf

Your ball is a charge on a green in four dimensions, and the green is threaded with magnetic fields. You can't hit it. You can only push it, with an electric field, for a moment, and the magnetic field turns every push sideways: by how much, and which way, depends on the field's two planes (what a 4D field is). The green is the plane x–y. The ball can also drift off it, into z and w, which you can't see except on the gauges: drift too far and it's lost.

How to play. Drag from anywhere on the green: the direction is the push, the length its strength. The white arrow is your push; the blue one is where the field will actually send the ball during it, worked out from the tile under the ball (it can't see the tiles ahead). Let go to push. The ball drifts, then coasts to rest against the gas. To sink it, stop it in the cup on the green, z and w both near zero (the green band on the gauges).

What the ball is doing

A charge pushed by a steady electric field E through a magnetic field F, slowed by the gas, settles to a steady drift v = −(F − γ)⁻¹E: in each of the field's two planes, turned nearly a right angle from the push, at the push's strength over that plane's rate. A stiff plane barely lets it move; a weak one lets it slide (the rough and soft rough). A field turning the other way turns the drift the other way (back rough).

The highway is a field of one plane, z–w, which turns nothing in the green: the green is its free sheet, and a push there goes straight, as if there were no field at all. The slant is a field of two planes, x–z and y–w, each half in the green and half out of it: a push along the green drifts the ball out of it, into z and w. Come back by drifting the other way. The left field turns both its planes at the same rate (all left).

It circles in both planes and stays within 2|v|/b of where it was, for ever, since there's no field line to run along. In ours a magnetic bottle leaks out of its ends, which is half of why fusion is hard; a uniform 4D magnet of two planes holds a charge perfectly (on its own: a hot crowd of charges and its own fields are another matter). The selftest checks it, and the drift, and the highway's run-away when there's no gas.
