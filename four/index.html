FOUR

A universe with four dimensions of space, worked out: what its light does, what its atoms are, which molecule is its water, and how they hold together. It's computed wherever it can be, with solvers written for any dimension, and every solver is checked first on our own world, where the answers are known.

Start here: paper 1 · a periodic table for four dimensions

  • The argument and the main results in six pages. Gauss's law gives a big, flat four-dimensional world no atoms, so the law between charges is pinned at 1/r. Then the atoms are solved: rows of 2, 10, 10, 28 and 28, a hardest-held electron that belongs to a full d shell rather than a noble gas, Hund's first rule broken, neutral atoms that pull from afar, and Pauli's exclusion derived rather than assumed.

The model

Where the numbers come from: every result is computed by the solvers, checked against our own world first, and stated with what it rests on.

The periodic table · 140 elements

  • Every element to 140, as the solver finds it, in rows of 2, 10, 10, 28, 28 and 60 (an f shell of 32), with our own table beside it.
  • Inside each atom: its subshells in profile, and any orbital cut by any of the six planes of four-dimensional space.
  • 88 do what one of ours does and take its name, a letter on: iydrogen, darbon, pxygen. 52 are new.

Making sense of it

Widgets for building an intuition: things to turn, slice, fly and play. Some run on the model's own code; others are built on the same geometry. They live here, on kata and in the arcade.

Charges, light and atoms

  • FieldsCharges and magnets under the pinned law: field lines from empty space, a neutral atom felt from outside, a charge turning in two planes at once.
  • The paper's widgetsChoose a law and watch atoms vanish or appear; turn screening on and off; feel a neutral atom's far pull.
  • Hund's rule, brokenWhy two d electrons here would rather pair their spins: the orbitals, their angles, and the level ladder.
  • ORBITALkataHydrogen's electron cloud in four dimensions: four p orbitals, nine d, sixteen f, and your slice through the nucleus.
  • Lumen⁴Light beside our own: 1/r³ falloff, a flash with a negative wake, a round hole's first dark ring at 1.43 λ/D.
  • IRIS⁴Walk a four-dimensional landscape after rain and shape the rainbow by turning into w.
  • PlasmaA magnetic field made of two planes, frozen into flowing gas, and a dynamo that grows faster than ours.
  • WAVELABkataWater waves on a surface with three directions in it: a pebble dropped out of your slice, rings appearing from nowhere.
