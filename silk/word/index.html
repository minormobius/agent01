    and now the charts that actually work

    The web above is a bad chart. Its whole angular axis is twelve categories, its radial axis is one number, and it has about six hundred legible cells for thirty-nine thousand words — what you are actually reading is a hundred and fifty labels and a density field. It is worth looking at because it is an object, not because it is efficient. These three are efficient.

    How this was made

    The repo comes down as one CAR file from the account's PDS (com.atproto.sync.getRepo) — 91 MB, 301,721 blocks, parsed by a ninety-line CAR + DAG-CBOR reader with no dependencies. Every block whose $type is app.bsky.feed.post is a post; the Merkle tree never has to be walked.

    Tokenising uses /rite/lexicon's stopword list, read from its source so the two surfaces cannot drift, plus three fixes that each changed the answer here. Pasted bsky.app links had put bsky, app and profile in the top ten most-used words. [a-z'] does not match a curly apostrophe, so every phone-typed don't was splitting into don + t — which is why don ranked seventh. And contractions needed folding onto their stems before the stopword list could see the it in it's. Before those three, the top of this vocabulary was punctuation and a URL scheme.

    The topics are not from an embedding service. They are PPMI over co-occurrence within a session — a run of posts with no gap longer than forty-five minutes, plus anything sharing a thread root — reduced by power iteration and clustered with spherical k-means. Two things had to be got right for any structure to appear at all. The context unit had to be the session and not the post: at six content words a post, post-level co-occurrence is almost all zeros and ones, and the first run produced a flat eigenvalue spectrum with six of eight clusters empty. And the leading eigenvector had to be thrown away, because the first component of any PPMI matrix is the frequency axis — keeping it made every "topic" a frequency band.

    Nine hundred words carry the embedding. The other thirty-eight thousand get a wedge by the company they keep: across the sessions a word appears in, sum the wedge membership of the embedded words beside it and take the argmax — but of the lift, not the raw count. On a raw vote the hub cluster wins everything, because being everywhere is what makes it the hub; the first build put 37,829 of 39,554 types into it.

    No post text leaves the build. The data file carries word types, counts, document frequencies, first and mean dates, and a wedge number — a bag of words, which is what the chart is about. Shipping the prose would be a different and much more exposing artefact for no gain.

    Your own web is built on your own machine. Type a handle and the whole pipeline above — the CAR reader, the tokeniser, PPMI, the eigendecomposition, k-means — runs in a Web Worker in your tab. The repo is fetched from that account's data server directly; it never touches this server, and neither does the result. It is the same engine.mjs that produced the committed example, not a second implementation, and the selftest asserts the two agree.

    Two things follow from that which are worth knowing. It is a real download: a 50,000-post account is about 90 MB. And a repo only has what its server will hand out — com.atproto.sync.getRepo is public on the main Bluesky hosts, but a self-hosted PDS can decline, and then there is nothing to read.
