A Theory of the Gate

Why a check behaves unlike everything else a turn knows, what a wrong assertion costs, and when to build the thing twice instead

Design stage · no runs executed · ken/graph/gate.mjs · 2 hypotheses · interactive
Abstract

WP3 gave an unattended chain a floor: a share of defects nothing downstream can see any more, because what a turn can see decays at λ per handoff. This paper asks what a check does to that floor, and the answer rests on one property.

A check does not attenuate. Everything else a turn knows about its ancestors arrives discounted by λk−1; an executable check arrives at full strength, because it is re-run rather than remembered. Two things follow. A defect inside its coverage contributes nothing to the floor at any gap. And the final turn's mistakes, which in WP3 had nobody after them, now have somebody.

That would end the matter if checks were free and correct. They are neither, and the premise this paper was handed says how: it is at least as hard to verify as to design. A check that covers more asserts more, and the assertions added last are the ambitious ones. So the gate needs an unsoundness u and a difficulty tail γ, and the defects a wrong assertion creates are certified as passing by the very mechanism that made them.

Density is then (1−c)(1+βc)·M + u·cγ, which gives a stopping point rather than a target: specify up to c* = (M / γu)1/(γ−1) and no further. It also gives an uncomfortable corollary. M falls as λ rises, so improving the briefing lowers the optimal amount of specification. Context and verification are substitutes.

When a sound check cannot be written, build the thing twice and treat disagreement as the detector. That has a floor too, and it is not p². Knight and Leveson rejected the independence assumption at the 99% level for human programmers, and one model sampled twice has less reason to differ than two universities do. The crossing between the two strategies is the generalizable pattern, and which side of it you are on is a measurement nobody here has made.

1The shape was already in the catalogue

The pattern this paper was handed runs: break the problem into two efforts, have two turns build verification tools for those efforts, have two successors build to pass them, and let an integrator glue the result together. That is four stages of widths 1, 2, 2, 1, which is six turns, which is standard in WP2's catalogue with lane wiring.

Nothing about the graph is new, and that is the useful part. Every number WP2 computes for standard carries over unchanged: depth 3, peak width 2, sink ken 0.5, an automorphism group of order 2. What is new is what the turns are for, and WP2's nine roles cannot express it, because those are read off degree and degree cannot tell a specification from an implementation. Under lane wiring all four middle turns are relays, whichever of the two duties they carry.

So a turn has a role, which its in- and out-degree force, and a duty, which the assignment chooses. The two are orthogonal, and a run is not described until both are stated.

One decision does move the roles, and finding that out is the reason this table is generated from roles.mjs rather than typed. Give the builder an edge from the setup turn, so it can read the original problem beside its check, and four of the six roles change: the specifiers become delegates and the builders become funnels. What reads like a preference in §8 is a different graph.

2A check does not attenuate

Every input a turn receives about its ancestors is discounted by λk−1, which is what produced WP3's floor. An executable check is the exception, and the reason is mechanical rather than clever: it is re-run, not remembered. A check written at turn 2 is exactly as sharp at turn 6 as it was at turn 3, because turn 6 does not recall it. Turn 6 executes it.

Two consequences, and the second is the one WP3's model had no way to produce.

  • No decay. A defect inside the check's coverage is detected at any gap, so it contributes nothing to the floor.
  • No last turn. WP3's floor is partly S(0) = 1: the final turn's mistakes have nobody after them, so no chain length drives density to zero. A check has somebody after them. It runs on the integrator's output too.

Fagan established the practice of counting defect removal as a rate rather than describing it, which is the move this whole model depends on. What is added here is the observation that the rate has a gap argument, and that for a check the gap argument is constant.

That claim is directly testable and is H10. It rides on H9's seeded-defect machinery with one turn added, so it costs twelve turns on top of a run already budgeted. It also carries its own null: a check nothing trips is flat at zero, and the loop ledger's 59 of 89 turns at the probe ceiling is what that looks like from the outside.

3The check can be wrong, and it certifies itself

An assertion that states the wrong thing does more damage than no assertion at all. It turns a would-be-correct implementation into a defect, and it marks that defect as passing. The failure is invisible to the mechanism that created it, which is why it needs a parameter of its own rather than a caveat.

The premise this paper was handed says what shape that parameter has. It is at least as hard to verify as to design, so a check covering more asserts more, and the assertions you add last are the ambitious ones you are likeliest to get wrong. Two numbers, then: u, the share of defects a complete specification would itself introduce, and γ, how much harder the last assertions are than the first. A third, β, covers what happens to the parts nobody specified, since the pattern instructs the implementer to optimise for the check. That is Goodhart's law arranged deliberately rather than stumbled into, and Manheim and Garrabrant's taxonomy names this variety regressional.

D(c) = (1 − c)(1 + βc)·M + u·cγ M is WP3's ungated density; the second term is what the gate itself put there

4Where to stop specifying

The gate-created term is superlinear whenever γ > 1, which gives an interior optimum and two statements worth arguing with.

The first assertions are always worth writing. At γ > 1 the derivative at c = 0 is −M, so a little coverage always pays. The question was never whether to check.

c* = ( M ⁄ γu )1/(γ−1) where to stop, at β = 0
Figure 1. Density against coverage, split into what the check misses and what its own wrong assertions create. The total falls and then rises: past c* more specification makes the artefact worse, and the second term is the reason.

The comparative statics are all interpretable, and one of them is unwelcome. A better check-writer, meaning a lower u, should specify more. A steeper difficulty tail moves the stopping point without moving it monotonically, because a very large γ concentrates all the difficulty in the final assertion and lets you specify nearly everything. And M falls as λ rises, which gives the result this paper would least like to be true.

Improving the briefing reduces the optimal amount of specification. Context and verification are substitutes rather than complements: fix the handoff and some of your tests stop paying for themselves, because the chain would have caught those defects anyway. WP3's conclusion was that wiring is the first lever; this is the bill for that, and it says a programme that improves λ should expect to write fewer checks, not more.

The bare version of the comparison is worth stating on its own, because it is the premise turned into an inequality. D(1) = u and D(0) = M, so specifying everything beats specifying nothing exactly when u < M: you have to be likelier to get an assertion right than the chain is to leave the defect anyway.

5When a sound check cannot be written

Build the thing twice, independently, and treat disagreement as the detector. This spends a whole second implementation rather than one specification turn, and it asks nothing of anyone's ability to state what correct means in advance.

Its floor is not p². Knight and Leveson had twenty-seven versions written independently from one specification and rejected the assumption of independent failure at the 99% level. Eckhardt and Lee give the model that replaces it: inputs vary in difficulty, versions fail independently given the difficulty, and therefore fail together on the hard ones. Correlation enters as the variance of that difficulty, and agreementFloor() implements that model rather than the independence one, because the independence one is known to be false.

6The pattern, generalized

Putting the three on one scale answers the question this paper was asked. Each independent attempt carries the density an unattended effort carries, so what survives build-twice is the share both get wrong together, and the three rows become comparable.

Figure 2. The three strategies against error correlation, at u = 0.3, γ = 2, c = 0.6. Specify-first does not depend on ρ; build-twice rises with it and meets the ungated line at ρ = 1.

So the generalizable pattern is not the six-turn shape. It is a choice between three strategies, decided by whether you can write a sound check more reliably than you can do the work twice.

  • Ungated. Every turn builds. Correct when the work is cheap relative to any check worth writing and the defects are ones a later turn can still see.
  • Specify-first. One turn writes an executable check, which then runs at every later turn and on the integrator. Correct when u sits below M, which is to say when you can state what correct means more reliably than the chain can stumble into it.
  • Build-twice. Two independent attempts; disagreement is the detector. Correct when stating correctness in advance is the hard part, and only while ρ stays low enough that the two attempts fail differently.

The quantity that decides between the first two deserves a name, and it is the one this theory most needs and least has: the verification discount, the cost of checking an answer over the cost of producing one. When it is well under 1, spend width on definitions. When it approaches 1, a check that costs as much as the work has no advantage over a second attempt, and the honest move is the one the premise implies.

8Decisions the profile leaves open

Fixing the shape and the duties still does not fix the run. Three wiring decisions remain, and the first version of this pattern left all three implicit, which would have made two runs of it incomparable.

Table 4. Decisions the profile does not settle, each of which changes what the run measures.
The profile does not settleWhich changes what the run measures
does the builder see the problem statement, or only the checkONLY THE CHECK is maximum independence and makes coverage the whole specification. BOTH lets the builder notice an unsound assertion, and changes four of the six derived roles: the specifiers become delegates and the builders become funnels
does the builder run the check, or only the integratorthe builder running it is a tighter loop and also self-certification against a possibly-wrong check, which is the unsoundness term with the blindfold on
do the two lanes see each other`s checkslane wiring says no, which preserves the pairing WP2 prices at a 40% saving but forgoes the cross-check that would measure unsoundness for free

The first is structural rather than stylistic, as §1 noted: it changes four of the six derived roles, so two runs differing only in that answer are not two runs of one design.

The second is the sharpest for what gets measured. A builder that runs its own check has a tight loop and is also certifying itself against a possibly-wrong check, which is the u term with the blindfold on. A held-out scorer is the only construction that measures u at all, and H11 requires one.

H10 measures the first claim, that a check does not attenuate, for twelve turns on top of H9. H11 puts the three strategies against each other over the same tasks, paired, at eighteen turns per task, and yields ρ as a by-product of running the build-twice arm at all. Both are in the register, and neither can start before H8 gives λ, since M is a function of it.

10Limits

  • No runs. Every number here is arithmetic over parameters that are assumed, and two of them have no instrument even in design.
  • Coverage folds together what a check examines and how reliably it detects a defect there. Splitting them would add a parameter and change nothing in the comparative statics, which is the argument for folding them, not a proof that folding them is right.
  • A defect inside coverage is treated as caught with certainty. Flaky and partial checks exist, and they enter here as lower coverage rather than as their own failure mode.
  • The unsoundness term assumes a wrong assertion always produces a surviving defect. In practice some wrong assertions are so wrong that the builder notices, which this model has no way to represent and which would make specify-first look better than it does here.
  • β is carried through the algebra and set to zero in every figure, because no measurement would distinguish it from u at present. Its effect is to move c* down, so the stopping points in Table 2 are upper bounds.
  • The Eckhardt–Lee model gives correlated failure a single parameter. Real correlation varies by input and by failure kind, and a single ρ will average over that.
