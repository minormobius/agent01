Does Unattended Work Improve With Turn Count?

A protocol, deliberately unfinished, whose blanks are the deliverables of the curriculum

Status: SKELETON · not registered · no data collected under it
Abstract

A budget-governed agent loop was run for ninety-nine turns and produced no measurement of the quality of its output. This protocol sets out how to run it again so that it does. It is written in the Stage 1 registered-report form, with hypotheses, design and analysis plan fixed before data collection, because the failure mode this programme is most exposed to is a flexible analysis of an expensive run.

It is deliberately incomplete. Six blanks are marked in the text, each requiring a quantity that does not yet exist: a defined construct, an established reliability, a calibrated judge, a powered sample size, a stopping rule, and an outside signal. Each blank is the deliverable of a curriculum unit. The protocol is not registrable, and the run is not fundable, until all six are filled. That ordering is the point of the document.

1Background, and the state of the evidence

The apparatus exists and is disabled. A ticket graph, a budget governor, a contagion firewall and four chain-reaction workflows were built and run; ninety-nine work orders were issued over roughly fifty-three hours in August 2026, of which eighty-nine recorded a completed turn. The programme's stated deliverable was the curve: quality against turn count, measured, over many artifacts.

Two later bake-off runs met the same fate by a different route, and the house standard was written from all three. This protocol assumes that standard and does not restate it; the rules most load-bearing here are R4 on reporting discrimination, R5 on replication within a cell, and R10 on treating an uncollected signal as a failed run.

No such measurement was produced. The dependent variable was never recorded once. The situation as of the last recorded turn:

Two things follow, and it is important to keep them apart. The first is that the study was never run, in the sense that the quantity of interest was never observed; the pilot is therefore evidence about the apparatus, not about the question. The second is that the one signal which did fire could not have answered the question anyway: a measure taking three values, two thirds of them at its maximum, has almost no variance to explain in the region where the interesting turns live.

What the pilot data cannot support

A faint negative trend in probes across the run (slope ≈ −0.0014 per turn) is not statistically meaningful at this sample size, and probes is in any case a liveness signal rather than a quality measure. The near-doubling of gate failures between the first and second halves is real but confounded: later work may simply have been harder, and nothing in the record distinguishes those explanations. Neither observation should be cited as evidence that quality declined. They are reasons to run the study properly, not substitutes for having run it.

2Hypotheses

Stated before the fact, and stated so that they can lose.

H1 (monotone improvement). Mean artifact quality is non-decreasing in turn count over the range studied.

H2 (saturation). Quality rises and then plateaus, with the plateau reached well inside the budgeted range — the "where does unattended work stop paying" hypothesis, and the one the programme was built to test.

H3 (overoptimisation). Quality rises, peaks, and then declines with continued turns, as gate-satisfying effort displaces value-producing effort. This is the reward-overoptimisation shape reported for a closely analogous setting , and predicted here on independent grounds by the multitask result .

H2 and H3 are the live competitors. H1 is included because it is what the system's own gates would have reported, and the study is partly an audit of that report.

The pre-specified discriminator. H2 and H3 are distinguished by the sign of the quadratic term in a quality-on-turn-count regression over the second half of the range, with the decision rule fixed in §4 before any quality data is seen.

3Design

3.1 Unit of analysis, and the thing being varied

The unit is the artifact, not the turn. Turn count is the manipulated variable: artifacts are developed for a randomly assigned number of turns drawn from a pre-specified grid, and quality is measured once, at the end, by raters blind to the turn count that produced it.

This is the design decision that matters most, and it is worth being explicit about why it differs from the obvious approach. Measuring the same artifact repeatedly as it develops confounds turn count with the artifact's own trajectory and invites the rater to reward visible change. Randomising turn count across artifacts and measuring once buys a clean between-subjects contrast at the cost of needing more artifacts — which is precisely the trade Unit II's power analysis is for.

3.2 Conditions

Table 2. Arms. The control arm is not optional: a comparison without it cannot distinguish "more turns helped" from "these tasks were easy".

The restart arm is the one most likely to be cut for budget and the one most likely to produce the study's most interesting result. It should be defended.

3.3 Blinding

Raters see the artifact and nothing else: no turn count, no commit history, no ledger, no gate outcome. Where a model judge is used, the same applies to its prompt, and artifacts are presented in randomised order with pairwise comparisons run in both orders so that position bias can be estimated rather than assumed away .

4The six blanks

What follows is the part of the protocol that cannot yet be written. Each blank names the quantity required, the unit that produces it, and the reason the study is not registrable without it.

B6 deserves emphasis because it is the one that cannot be solved by more care. Every other blank is a technique. B6 requires a person who is not us to look at the output and say something, and no amount of internal rigour substitutes for it. The programme's own operator notes have already identified this as the missing signal; the protocol simply refuses to proceed without it.

5Analysis plan

To be fixed in full once B1–B4 are closed. The skeleton:

  • Primary. Quality regressed on turn count, with artifact-family random effects and a pre-specified quadratic term. H2 versus H3 is decided by the sign and confidence interval of that term, under a decision rule written before unblinding.
  • Secondary. The restart contrast at matched total cost; gate-pass rate as a function of turn count, reported separately from quality, so that the divergence between them is visible rather than averaged away. That divergence is the study's most likely publishable finding.
  • Measurement model. Pairwise verdicts fitted with Bradley–Terry , with output length as a covariate so that strength is estimated holding verbosity fixed .
  • Correction for attenuation. Effect estimates reported both raw and corrected using the reliability from B2, with both shown.
  • Everything else is exploratory and will be labelled as such in the report. Any analysis not listed here is exploratory by definition, including analyses that seem obvious later .

6Outcome-neutral checks

Conditions that must hold for the data to be interpretable at all, regardless of which hypothesis it favours. These are checked first, and a failure halts the analysis:

  • The comparison graph is connected, so every artifact is linked by some chain of comparisons. An unlinked artifact has no estimable rating.
  • Inter-rater agreement meets the floor established in B2. Below it, the raters are measuring different things and the scale does not exist.
  • The control arm is distinguishable from the top arm. If one turn scores like twenty, either the task set is too easy or the measure has a ceiling, and in both cases the study is uninformative about the question rather than informative about the answer.
  • Position-bias effect size is smaller than the smallest quality difference the study claims to detect.

7Kill criteria

Stated in advance, because a programme that cannot say what would end it is not an experiment:

  • No outside signal by the end of Unit VII. If B6 cannot be closed, the run is not worth its budget and the programme reduces to the curriculum.
  • Reliability below the floor after two rubric revisions. If the construct cannot be measured consistently by people who agree on what they are looking for, it is not yet a construct.
  • Power analysis demanding more artifacts than the budget affords, with no reduction in the smallest interesting effect that remains honest. Redesign, or stop; do not run underpowered and hope.

The retrospective analysis of the pilot, meaning the reconstruction of per-turn history from the ledger and an account of why two of three signals never fired, is not a phase. It is a Unit I exercise, and it is listed there deliberately: it is worth doing with the measurement vocabulary in hand and close to worthless without it.

9Grounds for publication

Not a positive result. A curve of any shape, produced under a pre-registered design with a calibrated instrument and an outside signal, would be unusual in this area on methodological grounds alone, since most reported evaluations of unattended agent work supply a number without establishing that it measures the construct , and without a control arm.

The most likely genuinely novel finding is the divergence: gate-pass rate rising while measured quality does not, quantified, on a real system, with the mechanism identified in advance by a model from 1991 . That is a small, checkable, and honest contribution, and it is the one this protocol is designed to be able to detect.
