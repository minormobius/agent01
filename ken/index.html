“Beyond your ken” — outside the range of what one can see, or know, or judge. the working definition of the problem

§Aims and scope

This is a study programme with a website attached, not a journal with contributors. It exists because a specific question turned out to have a rigorous literature that nobody had assembled: how do you know whether work you did not supervise was any good?

The question is usually treated as new. It is not. It is the intersection of five mature fields — psychometrics, the design of experiments, the economics of delegation, the study of proxy failure, and scalable oversight — which are individually formidable and which substantially do not cite one another. The programme's wager is that the intersection is empty for historical rather than intellectual reasons, and that assembling it is worth a year.

The organising claim, which everything here is arranged to test:

Your abstraction ceiling is your measurement ceiling. You cannot delegate at a higher altitude than your ability to tell whether what came back was any good — so an agent that seems unable to climb is very often an evaluator that cannot follow.

Two documents follow. The curriculum is the foundation: six units, real primary sources, one mathematical model to be derivable in each, and one foundational experiment to be reproduced from scratch. The protocol is the experiment the programme is ultimately for, written as a registered report and deliberately left with six holes in it — because each hole is a quantity that one of the units produces, and publishing the holes is more honest than filling them with guesses.

§Editorial: ninety-nine turns, and nothing measured

In August 2026 a budget-governed agent loop in this repository issued ninety-nine work orders over about fifty-three hours. Eighty-nine of them recorded a completed turn. The programme's stated deliverable was a curve — quality against turn count — and the number of quality scores it produced was zero.

That is not a malfunction. Every component behaved as written. Three quality signals were specified; one of them, a liveness probe, was implemented and fired. The other two — a rubric and an adversarial check — were never built, and the judge that was to combine them was correctly configured to withhold a score while uncalibrated. So it withheld, ninety-nine times, exactly as instructed.

The signal that did fire is worth looking at, because it is a textbook object. Across eighty-nine turns it took three distinct values — 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 — and sat at its maximum in fifty-nine of them. Two thirds of all turns are tied at the top. Whatever else that measure is, it cannot rank the turns that matter, because among the ones that matter it does not vary. A century-old literature has a name for this and a formal account of why the reliability of such a measure collapses precisely where you need it.

The temptation is to read the record for a trend anyway. There is a faint downward drift in the probe across the run, and gate failures nearly doubled between the first and second halves. Neither will bear weight: the drift is not significant at this sample size, the probe is not a quality measure, and the gate failures are confounded with task difficulty, which nothing in the record controls for. Reporting them as evidence of decline would be exactly the error this programme exists to stop making.

What the pilot does establish is narrower and more useful. The apparatus runs. The instrument does not exist. And the order of work implied by those two facts is the reverse of the intuitive one: there is no point spending model budget on a longer run until there is something capable of telling you what the run produced. Hence a curriculum before a protocol, and a protocol before a single further turn.

§Standing notes

On the bibliography. Every work cited on this site is real, and every citation is a key into a shared bibliography module that an offline test checks on every build: a dangling reference fails the build rather than rendering a broken bracket at a reader. Nothing here is cited that is not intended to be read.

This is worth a machine check rather than good intentions, because the site next door at wormhole.mino.mobi generates entire fictional literatures — plausible papers, plausible citations, plausible fields, all of it invented. The two surfaces are a matched pair. One is a demonstration of how easily an apparatus of scholarship can be counterfeited; the other had better be able to prove it is not counterfeit.

On status. Both documents are living. The curriculum will be revised as units are completed, and the protocol's blanks will be closed one at a time. Neither is a finished claim, and the dates on the revisions are the honest record of pace.
