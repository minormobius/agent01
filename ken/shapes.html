Shape Explorer

Every org chart a turn budget admits, and what each one costs

Runs ken/graph/*.mjs in the page — the same modules the selftests gate, not a copy

WP2 catalogued six shapes by hand at six turns. The catalogue generalises with no new idea: a layered plan is a composition of n, with the source and sink pinned to width one, so there are exactly 2n−3 profiles and they can all be enumerated.

The trade is isoperimetric and it is arithmetic rather than a finding. The interior holds n−2 turns over d−1 layers, so peak width ≥ ⌈(n−2) / (d−1)⌉, with equality exactly when the interior layers are balanced. Read it as volume against surface, with the output in time: n is what the run costs, depth is the wall clock if a layer runs at once, and peak width is how many agents must exist simultaneously. Fix the cost and the product is fixed with it.

2Ken and replication, which the trade leaves free

Two quantities move independently of depth and width, which is why the table is worth reading rather than eyeballing.

Sink ken is free at every depth. Briefing the last turn adds an edge from every other turn to it. That changes no width, no depth and no cost, and it takes sink ken to 1. What it does cost is merge load: at n = 12 the last agent's in-degree goes from 1 to 11, and it must now read the whole run. Free in turns is not free in context, and no digest fixes that.

Free replication is concave in depth. The largest orbit is the number of turns a run contains that are replicates by symmetry. It falls off slowly at first and then sharply: at n = 12 the balanced middle at depth 6 keeps 92% of the maximum replication at a fifth of the peak concurrency. The star is rarely worth its width.
