§Design arithmetic

  1. At two samples per cell, a two-cell contrast could detect only d = 2.80. The gap between near-total failure and near-total success. Even a perfectly discriminating instrument had no power in that design. lab §2 · design.mjs
  2. Pairing cuts a medium-effect comparison from 126 runs to 38 at ρ = 0.7, and 20 at ρ = 0.85. The saving is a factor of (1 − ρ) and costs nothing to take. lab §3
  3. For a fixed run budget, repeats do not buy precision. With R = tasks × repeats held constant the within-task variance term is σ²w/R whatever the split, so one repeat is optimal at every ICC. Repeats buy the variance estimate instead, which is a different and necessary thing. lab §4 · allocate()
  4. A 24-run variance pilot returns a 95% interval on ICC of [0.00, 0.80] at a true 0.5. Every split of those 24 runs gives the same width. Reaching ±0.16 costs 144 runs. WP1 §4 · simulate.mjs
  5. The same 24 runs detect a two-SD bimodal separation 69% of the time, and a three-SD one always. So the pilot was re-scoped from parameter estimate to model check rather than resized. WP1 §5

§H4 — exchangeability

Exploratory, and it cannot be otherwise. The data was collected for another purpose and its outcome columns had been looked at before these tests were written. A confirmatory version needs new runs. The outcome is turn duration, because the loop recorded no quality scores at all.

  1. 12 of 89 loop runs are flagged infrastructure failures, and the ledger scores them like real turns. Median 61s against 597s for confirmed real runs. Nine of the twelve carry gateFailed: false, so they are absent from the 17-failure count, and all twelve carry a probes score. h4.mjs partition()
  2. That 12 is a floor. The infra flag was added part-way through: 45 of 89 records predate it. A 120-second rule catches 11 of the 12 known cases and misclassifies 2 of 32 known-real ones; it flags 5 more among the unclassified. So the true count is somewhere between 12 and 17 — 13% to 19% of the run. INFRA_SECONDS
  3. The within-task order effect is one bead. Pooled slope −0.47 log-seconds per position (permutation p = 0.012, 20,000 shuffles). Drop lp-16d590 and it is −0.10 with p = 0.17. Six other leave-one-outs move it by less than 0.1. H4 is not refuted by the clean test. h4.mjs orderEffect()

The dropped bead is the one whose four runs went 774s, 430s, 39s, 37s — all four gate failures, failing progressively faster. That is a fact about the gate, not about exchangeability.
  1. Across the whole run, duration rose and then flattened: 213 → 500 → 798 → 546 → 550 seconds by block of twenty turns. The rise survives excluding gate failures and gets stronger (t = 6.19). §The bake-off, with the right error term

  1. I called this structure a split-plot last revision. It is not one. Each race runs a single brief, so there is no sub-plot factor and no second error term. It is a 2 × 3 factorial (harness × model) with two replicates, blocked by race. The consequence survives the correction: a cell contrast still needs the within-cell error term, and no bake-off report has used one, because none reported a standard error at all.   2. On duration, model matters and harness does not. Model F = 8.80 on 2 df, permutation p = 0.001. Harness F = 0.00, p = 0.999. Interaction F = 0.51, p = 0.606.   3. And the significant factor is the uninterpretable one.   4. Run-to-run duration varies by a factor of 1.46. Residual SD 0.378 in log seconds, 16 residual df. At two samples per cell the smallest cell-to-cell duration ratio that design could resolve is 2.88×. contrastSensitivity()

§The judging pass

The judge is the assistant, in one context, over notes relabelled by a seeded shuffle and scrubbed of harness, model and provider names. Verdicts were committed to git before the key was opened, so the order is checkable. The judge had read all twelve entries before any pair was shown, so the swap rate below is a lower bound on what an independent judge would show.

  1. The Bradley–Terry MLE does not exist for this data. Two entries went undefeated and one never won, which sends their strengths to ±∞. The winless case was a design gap — the two weakest entries were never compared — and the undefeated case is not fixable by adding pairs. A regularised fit (half a pseudo-win per observed pair) was required. bt.mjs, guard fired before any ranking was produced
  2. The judge flips on 18% of pairs, and every flip is a pair it had flagged as close. 5 of 28 pairs disagreed between their two presentations. That is order sensitivity concentrated exactly where discrimination runs out, which is the benign version of the finding. swapRate()
  3. Fifty-six comparisons cannot rank twelve entries. The fitted scale spans 2.63 log-odds end to end and the standard errors are about 0.9. Only the extreme pair separates at two standard errors; everything in between is one undifferentiated block. fitBradleyTerry()
Figure 3. The first judged ranking of these entries, unblinded. Intervals are ±1.96 SE. The two best are the same cell run twice, and so are two of the three worst — but with intervals this wide that is as likely to be luck as consistency.
  1. Judged quality is not significantly explained by model (p = 0.124) or harness (p = 0.155). The direction is inverted from duration: ds4-pro was the fastest model and produced the notes judged worst (mean θ −0.76 against +0.44 for ds4-flash). Neither contrast is significant at n = 12, so this is a direction, not a result. 20,000-permutation test on between-group variance of θ
  2. ICC of judged quality by cell is 0.413. Within-cell SD 0.710 against a total scale range of 2.63; two runs of the same cell differ by 0.84 on average, a third of the whole spread. This is the parameter every cost estimate on this site has been assuming, now measured once. And at 6 cells × 2 samples its own 95% interval is [0.00, 0.88], so it is an anchor rather than a number. varianceComponents() over the fitted θ

§The standard run, and a language test

  1. The run is now a fixed unit: six turns, four stages, width two. Setup, two parallel waves of two, cleanup. Each wave is a matched pair if both agents get the same state, which buys the pairing saving without any extra design. At ρ = 0.413 a large effect (d = 0.8) costs 30 runs or 180 turns; a medium one costs 74 runs or 444. A 96-turn budget reaches d = 1.07 and no further. the procedure · runshape.mjs
  2. Writing one page to the structural rules of ASD-STE100 took it from 27 violations per 100 sentences to zero. The longest sentence went from 50 words to 14. The rules are cheap to follow on a procedure, where every step wants to read the same. ste-lint.mjs, structural rules only
  3. And the two standards contradict each other. The tic lint failed the same page for sentence-length monotony, at 0.324 against a floor of 0.42 — which is precisely the over-correction the declauding register warns about, arrived at by following a different standard faithfully. Simplified Technical English mandates the flatness the other lint treats as a defect. Resolved by register: a page may declare itself a procedure, and the rhythm rules are then skipped. Procedures should be flat; arguments should not. prose-lint.mjs registerOf()

§The plan as a graph

  1. The run shape is now rewrite rules, on morph's laws. A cell expands into sub-cells; a wave takes its width from the condition bus rather than declaring it; and the only control flow is failure, so the budget stopping rule is the mechanism that ends the recursion rather than a separate check. Ask for ten runs on a 20-turn budget and the planner builds three and records why. run §5 · plan.mjs
  2. Probe and build are one interpreter behind a flag, so the cost estimate cannot drift from the plan. This is morph's resolve law and it is the one worth importing: a cost table maintained beside a runner eventually disagrees with it, and the same code cannot. Asserted over six designs including two that fail. probeMatchesBuild()
  3. The derived layout found a wiring bug the hand-drawn figure would have hidden. Wave A fed wave B as a complete bipartite graph, every turn waiting on every turn, because the whole previous wave was passed as the predecessor. Drawn honestly, it was obviously wrong. Drawn by hand I would have drawn lanes, which is what I meant rather than what the code said. Each condition is now a lane, and the selftest asserts the crossing edges are absent. Builder.parallel()
  4. The layout is seed-independent to about one part in ten million. Positions agree across five seeds, so the seed only breaks the initial symmetry and the picture is a function of the graph. My selftest originally asserted the opposite — that a different seed would move it — which is the assumption a force layout invites and this one does not support. Adding a condition now costs no geometry at all: tree.js hand-places every box, and this places none. layout.mjs relax()

§Our own errors

  1. A fallback that caught more than it could repair answered a nonsense design by growing it. The budget fallback fired on any experiment failure, including a request for zero runs, and then built budget / 6 runs, turning an invalid design into a large valid-looking one. It now repairs exactly the failure it was written for and rethrows the rest. FALLBACKS.experiment
  2. Four edits to the worker's route map silently did nothing, and nothing broke. Each replacement targeted a string the previous failed edit was supposed to have created, and I never asserted that any of them applied. The routes worked anyway: Workers Static Assets resolves clean URLs itself, so the map was never load-bearing. Measured on the live site — /lab, /wp1, /log and /run all returned 200 while absent from it. The map is deleted and the selftest now refuses one. worker.js
  3. My Simplified-Technical-English lint was wrong twice on its first run. It called every run of four content words a noun cluster, so "One run costs six turns" failed. And it called every be-verb near an -ed word passive, so "a matched pair" failed. Both were caught by reading its own output on the page written to satisfy it. Fixing them moved the existing pages from 33–47 violations per 100 sentences to 19–29, so the first baseline overstated the problem by about half. ste-lint.mjs
  4. My separation guard checked only half the failure. It refused a Bradley–Terry fit when an item never won, and let through two items that never lost — the same divergence mirrored. The fit returned strengths spanning 17 log-odds with standard errors of 12, which read as numbers. Now both directions are checked, and the message says which direction and whether adding pairs can fix it. bt.mjs
  5. Excluding gate failures made an OLS return a slope of exactly zero with a standard error of exactly zero. The filtered subset left a constant column duplicating the intercept, and the normal equations returned a singular fit wearing the costume of a null result. Found by a sensitivity check rather than by the maths. globalDrift() now drops a zero-variance term and says it did. h4.mjs
  6. Title similarity alone matched a PsycEXTRA dataset stub to False-Positive Psychology, and an American Historical Review book review to Chandler. Both scored 1.0, because the title is the same. Filtering registry record types fixed both; 74 of 82 entries now resolve, and the other 8 say "unlinked" rather than pointing somewhere approximate. resolve-refs.mjs

§The org chart of a run

  1. Degree forces nine roles, and the count is an argument rather than a survey. Originate and merge cannot co-occur, and neither can report and split, so a node's duties are one in-duty crossed with one out-duty. Three by three, total on every node of every finite DAG. That is what makes an arbitrary shape staffable. WP2 §2 · ROLE_TABLE
  2. Exchangeability is a symmetry of the plan, so H4 has a structural half that needs no data. Two turns may be pooled exactly when an automorphism of the graph exchanges them. This does not make H4 true, since an agent can still drift inside an orbit. It settles which comparisons were ever estimable, before anything is run. WP2 §3 · poolable()
  3. A star run holds four replicates by symmetry and a chain holds none. Every orbit in a chain is a singleton, so no care in running one will produce a second. At the measured ρ = 0.413 the design effect turns four raw replicates into 1.787 effective ones against a chain's 1.000: 1.79× the replication for the same six turns and the same agents. This was not the point of the catalogue and it is the more useful half. effectiveReplication()
  4. Depth and ken ratio are orthogonal across shapes and collinear within one. Across the six-shape catalogue r = 0.05 and all four cells of the two-by-two are occupied. Inside a single run they move together, r = −0.966 in a chain, because a chain gets deeper and blinder at every step. So the separation is a between-shape claim and costs between-shape money. My first framing had this backwards and priced the cheap design as though it tested the hypothesis. WP2 §6 · collinearity()
  5. One shape escapes, and it is the one that was already free. briefed advances depth while the last turn keeps full ken, so its variance inflation is 1.171 against the chain's 14.758 — the only shape in the catalogue that separates the two covariates inside one run. That the cheap design and the free intervention are the same shape was luck, and is recorded as luck. collinearity()
  6. The cheapest manipulation in the programme so far: four edges and no turns. chain and briefed match at every turn but the last, where sink ken goes from 0.333 to 1.000. Copying the final agent on every intermediate turn costs nothing and moves the quantity under test by two thirds of its range. chainBriefedContrast()
  7. Two shapes with identical role censuses differ twelvefold in symmetry. standard and star are both one principal, four relays and one integrate. Depth 3 against 2, |Aut| 2 against 24. If a role census were the whole model they would behave alike, so the pair is a controlled test of whether counting seats is enough. catalogue()

§Prior literature

  1. This experiment was run in 1951, on undergraduates. Bavelas wired groups into fixed communication patterns and treated the pattern as the treatment; Leavitt ran circle, chain, Y and wheel and found the wheel fastest and most accurate and its peripheral members least satisfied. Shaw then reversed it for complex tasks. Our catalogue holds task difficulty fixed and would miss that interaction entirely, which is now a stated limit rather than a discovery waiting to happen. WP2 §1 · Leavitt 1951 · Shaw 1964

§Any n, and the trade

  1. The catalogue generalises with no new idea: a layered plan is a composition of n. Layer widths with source and sink pinned to one, so exactly 2n−3 profiles, all enumerable to about n = 16. The six shapes of WP2 are recovered exactly as members of the generated family, which the selftest checks rather than assumes. shape explorer · profiles.mjs
  2. The depth-width trade is isoperimetric and the floor is reached. The interior holds n−2 turns over d−1 layers, so peak width ≥ ⌈(n−2)/(d−1)⌉, with equality when the interior is balanced. Tight at all 52 depth-and-n pairs checked. Volume is what the run costs, depth is the wall clock, peak width is how many agents must exist at once. widthFloor()
  3. Free replication is concave in depth, so the star is rarely worth its width. At n = 12 the balanced middle at depth 6 keeps 92% of the maximum replication at a fifth of the peak concurrency. The last five turns of latency buy 8%. ladder()
  4. Sink ken is free at every depth; the price is merge load. Briefing changes no width, no depth and no cost. At n = 12 it takes the last agent's in-degree from 1 to 11, so it must now read the whole run. Free in turns is not free in context. buildProfile({briefed})
  5. The Pareto frontier held exactly 2n−4 points at every n from 5 to 14. Odd and even alike. An observation, not a theorem; the selftest asserts it under a label saying so, because if it breaks that is a finding rather than a test to relax. frontierOf()

§Ancestry, after hoop

  1. An agent's state is its precedents, so the pooling equivalence is ancestry, not orbit. An orbit is defined by in- and out-structure; an agent reads only the in-structure, because nothing downstream can affect what it does. Within all six shapes the two partitions coincide, so no published figure moves. The correction is to the justification. WP2 §11 · ancestry.mjs
  2. The catalogue run once yields 7.65 effective observations against a star's 1.79. A turn fed by one setup turn is the same condition in every shape, so thirteen turns across all six form one pooling cell. They sit in six runs and are clusters of unequal size, so the design effect takes Kish's Σm²/Σm = 35/13 = 2.69. No automorphism relates those graphs; the digest does not need one. poolAcross()
  3. A digest buys identity, not ken, and hoop already said so. hoop-archive/js/postal.js folds a quadtree into one region hash and states plainly that a Merkle tree does not speed up routing, the index does that, and the hash is purely for verifiable and forkable state. The same limit binds here. An agent handed a root instead of five reports learns only whether it has seen this state before. Briefing still costs in-degree n−1 and no hash makes that cheaper. hoop-archive/js/postal.js · NAV.md
  4. Arity has to be folded in before the children, or the digest collides. hoop mixes a node's own coordinates into the accumulator first. Here the analogue is in-degree: without it a turn fed by one parent and a turn fed by two parents of identical ancestry hash the same. Asserted directly. ancestryDigests()
  5. The digest converts into a rule on the runner, which is R15. A turn's brief must be a function of its ancestry and nothing else. Name the shape to the agent, or dispatch in a fixed order, or share a scratch file, and equal digests no longer mean equal inputs. The digest cannot detect that; only the rule prevents it. R15

§The self-audit

A pass over sixteen revisions of our own output, asking what the direction we ended up in makes wrong. Four of the six findings are faults in work we published.

  1. R14 was refuted by our own arithmetic and stood for six revisions. It mandated a shape with no stage wider than two, justified by the pairing saving. A matched pair is an orbit of size two; the same six turns as a star put four turns in one orbit, worth 1.787 effective replicates against the mandated shape's 1.415. The rule forbade the shape its own reasoning preferred. R14 now requires a run to declare its profile and report four invariants of it, rather than prescribing one. R14 · standard now v1.4
  2. WP1 proposed H2 and went six revisions without recording its first measurement. H2 claims ICC ≥ 0.5. We measured 0.413 with an interval of [0.00, 0.88] at revision 9, put it in this log, and never carried it back to the paper that asked for it. The estimate is on the wrong side, the interval decides nothing, and H2's own refutation condition needs an upper bound below 0.5, so the status is undecided rather than refuted. The addendum is dated and the status now lives in one place. WP1 addendum · register
  3. The roadmap asserted an impossibility and nothing objected. Unit IV read blocked while WP2, which needs it, read active. Every existing check on that figure was about geometry, acyclicity and links; none was about the claim the colours make. A node may not be active unless its prerequisites are, may not be done unless they are done, and may not be blocked when all of them are done. tree.js · ken.selftest.mjs
  4. The hypotheses were prose in two papers with no status anywhere. Working out what was still open meant reading both and this log. They are one list now, and a status of supported, undecided or refuted must name its evidence while an undecided one must not. Both directions are asserted, because the failure to guard against is the quiet upgrade. The first run of that check found a real gap: H7 was registered and WP2 never named it. register · hypotheses.mjs
  5. The protocol asked the loop's question, not ours. It was written for a turn-count curve needing all six blanks. H5 needs none of B1, B3 or B6, costs 180 turns paired, and its manipulation is four edges. So H5 is Study 1 and the turn-count curve is Study 2. The blanks still gate Study 2; the ordering changed, not the requirement. protocol, restated
  6. A third prose register was needed, and the lint was right to demand it. The generated register cards failed four rules at once. Two were structural and are format rather than writing, so reference joins procedure in being excused them. The other two were lexical and were real faults in prose we had just written, so they were fixed rather than exempted. REGISTER_EXEMPTIONS

§The size of the space

  1. There are 1,960 shapes on six turns and the layered family reached 18. One source, one sink, up to isomorphism. Counted twice by different code: brute force over every relabelling, and a refinement-restricted canonical form. 0.9%. Skip policies took it to 33, which is 1.7%. exhaustive.mjs · coverage()
  2. Only ten of those 1,960 are strictly layered. In every other one some turn feeds something more than one stage later. The layered restriction is not a mild simplification of the space; it is almost all of the space thrown away. summarise().layered
  3. The second counting method was wrong and only the first caught it. The refinement-restricted canonical form gave 122 at n = 5 and 3,274 at n = 6 against the true 98 and 1,960. Two bugs: a neighbour colour read from the wrong node, and colour classes ordered by discovery rather than by an invariant, so two isomorphic graphs got different canonical forms. A faster method that silently disagrees is the failure this site is about, and it was caught only because the slow ground truth existed first. refineMatrix()
  4. The 2n−4 frontier was an artefact of the generator, and the label was right. Published one revision ago as an observation rather than a theorem. Widening the family broke it. With skips off and no briefing the frontier is n−2, one point per achievable depth; briefing doubled it to the 2n−4 we published; skip policies dissolve the pattern entirely. WP2 §10, corrected
  5. The explorer's table and that artefact were the same fault. The table showed one row per depth, so the family looked like a single shape stretched. Within the narrow family the Pareto frontier is one shape per depth, so the display was faithfully reporting how little there was. It now shows every shape, sortable, with the count and the coverage on the line above. shape explorer
  6. Exact enumeration reaches seven turns and stops. A DAG on n nodes with a fixed topological order is an upper-triangular matrix, so the search is 2n(n−1)/2: 32,768 masks at six turns, 2,097,152 at seven, 268 million at eight. Six enumerates in about 120 ms and is what the page does live; above seven the page samples and says so. EXACT_LIMIT · searchSize()

§An edge nobody could see

  1. Every skip edge was drawn straight through the node it skips, so none of them were visible. The renderer put a bezier's control points directly below the source and above the target. On a graph one turn wide that is a vertical line, painted under the nodes and exactly over the adjacent edges covering the same span. A six-turn chain with 5, 9, 9 and 15 edges rendered as four identical pictures. Skip edges now bow sideways by an amount that grows with the span, and carry their own class. renderPlan() · shapes
  2. WP2's own central contrast was invisible in WP2's own figure. The paper argues that chain against briefed is the cheapest manipulation available, four edges and no extra turns. Those four edges are all skips, so the two panels of Figure 1 were the same drawing with different captions, and had been since the figure was published. WP2 Figure 1, corrected
  3. Nothing caught it because every layout check was about nodes. Seed independence, no overlapping boxes, depth pinned to the Kahn pass, positions to one part in ten million. Not one check asked whether an edge could be seen. The regression test now renders the four skip policies and requires four distinct drawings, that every multi-depth edge is marked, that no skip is drawn on its endpoints' column, and that no two edges trace the same path. Run against the old renderer it fails seven checks. plan.selftest.mjs, 56 → 98 checks
  4. And one of the new assertions was weaker than its own label. "Four distinct pictures" compared SVG source, which differed even on the broken renderer because the path count and endpoints differed while the lines overlapped. Distinct source is not distinct appearance. It is relabelled as the necessary-not-sufficient check it is, and the real visual test is that a drawing with skips must leave the single column a width-one chain otherwise occupies. plan.selftest.mjs

§Whether the plan is enforced

  1. The ken ratio is 1 by construction whenever an agent inherits its ancestry, and that was true from the revision the ratio was defined. Ken is the in-neighbourhood over the ancestry. Nobody asked when the numerator and the denominator could differ. They differ exactly when the plan withholds, and withholding needs a mechanism the plan does not have. visibility.mjs · WP2 §10b
  2. 1,960 shapes become 16 under lineage and 8 under sharing. A turn reads its worktree and its history whatever arrows were drawn. Under lineage the effective graph is the transitive closure of the drawn one; under sharing it depends only on the depth histogram. Sink ken takes exactly one value, 1.000, in both. Not a small effect: no variance in the quantity the site is named after. collapse()
  3. The H5 contrast is exactly zero unless the run is strictly isolated. chain and briefed both become the same fifteen-edge graph with the same sink ken. The 180 turns §7 prices would measure nothing at all, and the result would be void rather than null. auditRegime()
  4. The regime most people would call isolated is not the one that works. lineage — a worktree per lane, merged at joins — leaves 16 of 1,960. Only fresh context, fresh tree and handoff by explicit artefact preserves the question. R16 makes stating and demonstrating it a requirement, and the demonstration cannot be asserted: plant a marker in a turn a later one has no edge from, and check it cannot be produced. R16 · standard now v1.5
  5. Five revisions of shape work rested on it, unstated. WP2 §10b carries the correction and the three shape hypotheses now carry a requires field the register's own audit enforces, so a shape hypothesis cannot be added without naming the regime it needs. register · auditHypotheses()
