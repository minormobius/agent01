Hardware

This page is where the bill of materials goes. It is empty on purpose: the design record fixes the shape of every part, and this fixes the actual chips. Doing them in that order is what stops a shortlist from being an opinion.

Status: next round. Six questions are open, and each is written up with its candidates and its trade-off in §7 of the design record. Nothing has been bought.

What the architecture already fixes

These are settled, and any part that violates one is out regardless of price:

  • 13.56 MHz NFC, ISO 14443A. Not 125 kHz — a phone can neither read nor write those, which removes the entire phone-side pipeline. Why.
  • NTAG213 is the floor. Everything is sized to its 144 bytes, and a test fails the build if that ever stops being true. That is what makes a story cost 20¢.
  • An SD card, not on-board flash. Audio is megabytes per book; a 32 GB card holds ~3,100 hours and costs less than the flash it replaces.
  • Under two seconds from power to a working card. This is a hard requirement and it is the main thing standing between us and a Raspberry Pi. Why.
  • A recessed card nest that positions the card by geometry. Read range is 1–4 cm and orientation matters; a four-year-old aims badly. This is an enclosure requirement derived from the radio, and it is free now and expensive after the case is printed.
  • Volume is a potentiometer with an end stop, trimmed once to a maximum a child cannot exceed.
  • The audio output is a swappable stage behind one small interface, so Q1 can be answered late and changed cheaply.

The shortlist, unpriced

Carried over from the survey so the next round starts with candidates rather than a blank page. The trade-offs behind each are in §7.
