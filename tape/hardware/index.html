Hardware

Parts chosen, with the losing candidates and why they lost. Dropping the Bluetooth speaker unlocked the modern ESP32 line, and doing the power budget before buying anything disqualified the charger everyone reaches for first.

Yes, it can be portable. About 20 hours of stories on one 18650, and roughly 8 days between charges on an hour a day. The budget is live below — change the cell and watch it move.

The bill of materials

Rendered from parts.json, which is the only place it is written down. Every link is checked by tape/check-links.mjs — and the check is not “does it return 200”, because vendors retire and reuse product ids, so a dead link resolves cheerfully to something else. The check is does the page still say the part’s name.

Electrical

Power chain

The one non-obvious choice. A 3.7 V cell sags to 3.0 V when flat, so a 3.3 V rail taken straight off it browns out before the cell is empty, and an amplifier fed from it gets quieter as the evening goes on. Boosting to a steady 5 V costs about 13% of the runtime and removes both problems — the budget below assumes it.

Connections

The pins you may not have. The ESP32-S3 looks like it has 45 free GPIOs and it does not. The costly one: GPIO33–37 carry the octal PSRAM on any “R8” module, and they look completely free on the pinout drawing. GPIO22–25 do not exist at all — the numbering jumps from 21 to 26. The full list is in pinmap.js and the selftest fails if an assignment lands on one, which is cheaper than finding out with a multimeter.

Where it goes while a story plays

The cards

A deck of playing cards, a sheet of NFC stickers, and a biro. The card lives in the book — that is the whole idea, and it is better than anything a product designer would have arrived at: the pairing between the physical book and the recording is maintained by the object itself, in the place you will next look for it.

The stack

  • A standard poker deck, 63×88 mm. About $2.
  • An NTAG213 25 mm round sticker on the back. ~12¢ in hundreds.
  • The 13-character id written on it in biro. The alphabet has no I, L, O or U precisely so that a hand-written id reads back unambiguously.

Two things that will bite

  • Cards get stacked. A deck is a stack by nature, and two tags in the field is an anticollision event, not a read. The firmware counts targets and refuses to act on more than one — the box blinks rather than guessing. In the state machine.
  • Not every deck works. Paper, linen-finish and PVC are all fine. A foil or metallic-backed deck detunes the antenna and will not read at all. Test one card before you sticker fifty.

Treating the cards as consumable is the right instinct, and it is what the architecture already assumes: the recordings are the asset and they live on the SD card, backed up wherever you like. A chewed card is a 12¢ problem — write a new sticker, bind it to the same book, and the audio never moved.

The enclosure

Still open, but no longer unconstrained. The requirements that have accumulated from decisions elsewhere:

  • A recessed card nest that positions a 63×88 mm card by geometry. Read range is a few centimetres and orientation matters; a four-year-old aims badly, and the case is what makes that not matter. The PN532’s antenna sits directly under it, with no metal between.
  • A screwed battery compartment, not a friction lid. A protected 18650 in a child’s toy needs to be a deliberate job to get to.
  • The pot’s end stop reachable for trimming once, at build time, then left.
  • The speaker firing upward or forward, not into a table.
  • Big enough to be carried by a four-year-old with two hands, which is the actual size specification.

Still open

  1. MP3 or WAV as the studio’s output. Both play. MP3 at 64 kbps mono gives ~1,200 hours on the card and needs an encoder in the browser; WAV needs nothing but AudioContext and gives ~220 hours. Both are far more than a childhood.
  2. Enclosure material, above.
  3. Whether standby is worth more silicon. The PN532’s duty-cycled field is the largest idle load. A PN5180 with hardware card detection would roughly triple standby. At eight days it does not feel worth $9 and a harder build.
