The Pomological Watercolor Collection

Between 1886 and 1942 the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s Division of Pomology paid artists to paint fruit. Not fruit in general — this apple, picked on this day, in this orchard. Colour photography could not yet be trusted to record a cultivar, so the plates were the record: they illustrated the department’s bulletins, settled what a variety looked like, and were later used as evidence in plant variety protection claims.

Twenty-one of the twenty-six hands on this page belong to women, at a time when very few paid scientific posts did. Deborah Griscom Passmore alone painted over a thousand of them.

The paintings are held by the National Agricultural Library in Beltsville, Maryland. They are works of the federal government, so they are in the public domain — you can download, print, alter and sell any plate on this page.
